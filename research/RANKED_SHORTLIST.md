## This cycle (ninth research cycle)

The cycle's dominant finding is a **data determination that reverses the eighth cycle's answer**, and it unblocks the catalogue's only non-derivatives entry. No hypothesis was added, one was reinstated, one was downgraded, two external questions were resolved, and the CoinGecko access trap was closed.

1. **The eighth cycle's "no dated delisting record on free data" is reversed, and it was wrong for a methodological reason.** A dated delisting record for tokens a major venue has delisted **is** constructible on free data - not from a vendor field, but by derivation from the Binance public data archive (`https://data.binance.vision`, access 1, no key). The archive keeps a dated daily kline file for every USDⓈ-M perpetual symbol ever listed (998 distinct symbols, including contracts the venue has since delisted), and the terminal file of a contract that has stopped trading carries **volume = 0 and trade count = 0 with a frozen close**. So: listing date = first file with a non-zero trade count; last trading date = last file with a non-zero trade count. Verified against two independently announced venue delistings - LUNAUSDT's last real trade on 2022-05-12 (volume 70.7bn, 20.1m trades) against the venue's 2022-05-13 delisting, and OMGUSDT trading through 2025-01-20 and zero-volume from 2025-02-10, immediately after the venue's announced 2025-01-31 settlement (first announced 2024-12-06, postponed twice) - and against one re-listing (CVCUSDT is zero-volume through 2024 and trades again on 2026-09-15). Two construction hazards are recorded: the archive's **monthly** tree is incomplete for some symbols (ANTUSDT monthly ends 2023-05 while its daily series continues to 2024-05-28; CVCUSDT monthly ends 2024-09 while its daily series continues to 2026-09) and must not be used for lifetime questions; and a symbol's trading lifetime must be reconstructed as **segments**, not one interval, because re-listings occur. The eighth cycle read the archive as a *price* source and concluded it held no dated delisting record; the record was there, encoded as the *absence of trades*. A parallel spot tree with the same logic (BTCUSDT monthly from 2017-08) makes a spot implementation equally constructible.

2. **CRYPTO-EDGE-004 is reinstated: 45/100, confidence low, rank 3.** Its deferral was on a data ground and that ground has been removed; the entry's registered prediction has still never been tested, so the falsification-handling rule remains unengaged and it is not rejected. Two dimensions move, in opposite directions, and the total is unchanged. Free-data accessibility 2/5 -> 3/5: the essential input - a survivorship-aware point-in-time crypto universe - is now buildable, with exact listing dates and exact last-trading dates for 998 perpetual symbols; but not 4/5 or 5/5, because the published result's *market-capitalisation* screen still has no free history, so a free replication must substitute listing-age and trailing-volume screens and is a **replication, not an exact replication**. 3. **CRYPTO-EDGE-001 is downgraded to 58/100, confidence low** - evidence quality 3/5 -> 2/5 and no other dimension moves. Three independent 2026 sources now bear on the transferability of its dated-futures support without testing its registered claim. Garcia Seuma (arXiv:2608.03616, 5 August 2026) measures the October 2025 record cascade in flight and finds it deeply subcritical (lambda-hat approximately 0.1), the transition abrupt and first-order rather than critical, with the in-cascade signature in the liquidity sector - price-impact spikes of 1.2-3.5x regressed and 3.2-9.1x quoted, while open interest clears by 25-70% (-24.6% for Binance BTC, to 0.30-0.55 of baseline on Hyperliquid) - and shows the branching model failing as an account of the *pre-cascade state* on both of its falsifiable predictions at simulated power of at least 0.9; its companion (arXiv:2607.27070) finds the price signature present in five of seven cascades but silent in exactly the two sudden-news shocks, and inverting between the two largest events. Kitron and Wengrowicz (arXiv:2608.21888) find funding, basis, funding-clock, volatility and session conditioning **flat in every case** across 183 Binance pairs. Memon, Anklesaria-Dalal and Mishra (IJFMR 4(4):86388) find that direct comparisons of conditional means between up-shock and down-shock events generally **fail** to establish statistically significant directional asymmetry - which is the statistical form a conditional crash or reversal claim takes. The mechanism score is deliberately held: nothing new contradicts the market-structure description itself.

4. **The third non-derivatives family was found, and it is rejected on cost, not on data.** Short-horizon flow-conditioned reversal (Kitron and Wengrowicz 2026) has free, point-in-time inputs - confirmed present in the same archive (daily `bookDepth/` from 2023-01-01, `aggTrades/`, `trades/`) - and the strongest published result located this cycle: 90% of 183 Binance pairs carry significant 15-minute directional reversal under one matched, strictly out-of-sample walk-forward protocol against 2.7% of 187 US stocks and ETFs, in every focal coin-year since 2021, with a class-mean AUC gap of +0.031 as designed and +0.011 (95% CI [+0.008, +0.014]) under the most conservative accounting, surviving an artifact battery, an exact permutation null and a frozen six-month holdout, with the mechanism documented as compensated liquidity provision after aggressive taker flow. It fails because the **gross edge peaks near 1.3 bp per trade against a 5 bp round-trip cost** - "large enough to detect, too small to clear benchmark spot capture costs". This is the second instance in this catalogue of a robustly detected signal dying only on costs, and it is the cleanest external demonstration that detection robustness and tradability are separate axes in this asset class.

5. **Two external questions are closed.** (a) *CoinGecko*: the 365-day historical limit is a **paid-tier artefact** - CoinGecko's own API reference states that the Demo tier (free, keyless or with a free key) is restricted to the past 365 days, the paid Basic tier to two years, and only Analyst and above carry the full range - so no free key lifts it. CoinPaprika applies the same 365-day limit (verified by direct fetch: "Getting daily historical data before 2025-10-20 ... is not allowed in this plan"), and its `last_data_at` field is the aggregator's last observation, not a cessation date. (b) *CRYPTO-EDGE-001's mandatory open-interest baseline* is now **access 1**, not access 2: the archive's `daily/metrics/` gives 5-minute open interest per symbol continuously from 2020-09-01 to the present (366 daily files for BTCUSDT in 2024, 365 in 2025, 292 in 2026 through 19 September), covering the whole post-July-2024 window. Historical liquidation notionals are **not** archived, so P1's "sell liquidations as a share of open interest" is not directly computable - but the open-interest *clearing* is the documented observable footprint of forced unwinding, and it is now computable at 5-minute resolution.

6. **The conditional funding-extreme split is still not replicated, and its prior is now lower.** No independent replication or refutation of Shek (2026, SSRN 7520320) was located. Two new adjacent 2026 results lean against it: the Memon et al. finding above, which is the closest published setting to Shek's headline statistic, and the flat funding-and-basis conditioning of Kitron and Wengrowicz. Single-source risk is therefore **not** discharged and the deferral stands, with the single-source risk elevated rather than reduced.

7. **Standing prior, extended.** The crash process in crypto is documented to be first-order and shock-driven in its two largest recent instances, with the observable footprint in open-interest clearing and price impact rather than in any pre-crash state variable. Any new candidate in the carry and crowding family must now answer two questions that were not on the list last cycle: *what in the state variable is not already a proxy for the common factor*, and *does the candidate survive on the observable footprint rather than on the precursor*. And no candidate in this family may be promoted on dated-futures evidence alone.

1. **A preregistered external test of the funding-extreme contrarian thesis failed with the wrong sign**, so CRYPTO-EDGE-002 is rejected rather than downgraded. The test (Binance USDT perpetuals, 8 symbols, 17,910 symbol-days, December 2019 to July 2026) used this catalogue's own signal construction — trailing-90-day percentile of daily funding, 10th/90th deciles, fixed before the run — and found 7-day LOW-minus-HIGH of -0.95% with a 95% block-bootstrap interval of [-3.20%, +1.39%] and sign consistency of 4 of 8 symbols against a required 5. Both point-estimate directions leaned the wrong way: HIGH-funding days were followed by higher returns (+3.30% vs +2.35% at 7 days; +12.61% vs +4.83% at 30 days). A momentum-flavored variant was also tested and not promoted, so both signs are closed. This closes the catalogue's largest evidence gap for CRYPTO-EDGE-002, which until now rested only on practitioner anecdote.
2. **The same reversal hits CRYPTO-EDGE-001.** A preregistered test of the perp-basis level on Binance perpetuals (17,899 symbol-days) found the 7-day LOW-minus-HIGH at -2.61% with an interval of [-4.88%, -0.12%] excluding zero *on the wrong side*: high-premium days were followed by +4.20% vs +1.59% at 7 days and +14.8% vs +6.1% at 30 days. This is the closest available test of the entry's directional claim, it covers the post-July-2024 window that no published sample covers, and it contradicts the mean-return form of the prediction. CRYPTO-EDGE-001 is therefore restructured from a directional return claim into a **crash-conditionality** claim, with a mandatory volatility-matched baseline, and is downgraded from 73 to 63.
3. **CRYPTO-EDGE-003 is downgraded from 61 to 58** because its sign is now contested rather than merely uncertain: three independent sources place price discovery on the more heavily traded and typically higher-funding venue, and a preregistered cross-venue test found the high-premium venue was followed by *higher* returns (+3.17% at 7 days, CI [+0.51%, +6.08%], breadth 17/20, surviving a trailing-return trim and a concentration check). The same test found cross-venue dispersion *as a magnitude* to be noise at all three horizons. CRYPTO-EDGE-003's validation plan is reordered so the sign is estimated before any trade expression is specified.
4. **CRYPTO-EDGE-004 added at rank 3, 56/100, confidence low.** The catalogue's first non-derivatives entry: cross-sectional dispersion of crypto returns as a state variable that switches off cross-sectional momentum when the cross-section disagrees most. Published, methodologically explicit prior art (Zhang and Makgolo, April 2026); the blocking constraint is a free point-in-time crypto universe, not the idea.
5. **Standing prior recorded for the next cycle:** in crypto, crowding-style signals (funding extremes, elevated basis, cross-venue premiums) have now been contradicted in three preregistered tests, all in the same direction — they accompany continuation rather than exhaustion. Any new contrarian candidate in this domain must beat that prior before it is promoted.

## Statement
No empirical strategy validation (backtests, paper trading, forward tests, parameter sweeps) was performed in this repository. This is research documentation only. Published and preregistered results from external sources are cited as evidence about the world, not as results of any test run here. The seventh, eighth and ninth cycles read, fetched and checked external sources directly, including public API endpoints and public data archives whose availability, coverage and access limits were verified by hand; no return, Sharpe, drawdown or performance figure for any strategy in this catalogue was computed, and no strategy was executed. In the ninth cycle the only files fetched from the Binance public data archive were directory listings and a small number of single-day kline and metrics files, and they were read **solely** to determine data availability, listing and last-trading dates, and field definitions; no return, spread, price statistic or strategy result was computed from them.
