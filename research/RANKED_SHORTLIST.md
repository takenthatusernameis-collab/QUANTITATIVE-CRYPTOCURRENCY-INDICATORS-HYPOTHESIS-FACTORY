1. **A free, Binance-native, per-contract, per-event, side-attributed forced-liquidation record exists — on the COIN-M (inverse) tree — and no prior cycle found it.** The path is `data/futures/cm/daily/liquidationSnapshot/{SYMBOL}/{SYMBOL}-liquidationSnapshot-YYYY-MM-DD.zip`, with a `.CHECKSUM` beside every file. Enumerated over the whole tree: **118 symbol directories** (the COIN-M perpetuals BTCUSD_PERP, ETHUSD_PERP, SOLUSD_PERP, ADAUSD_PERP and the quarterly delivery contracts), **17,182 symbol-days**, all inside a publisher window of **2023-06-25 to 2024-10-14**. The schema is `time,side,order_type,time_in_force,original_quantity,price,average_price,order_status,last_fill_quantity,accumulated_fill_quantity`; every observed row is LIMIT / IOC / FILLED; timestamps are epoch milliseconds at 1-30 second spacing, so the record is per-event, not a 5-minute snapshot. Access class 1: free, public, no key.

2. **Three construction hazards inside it, all silent-failure, all found before any interpretation.** (i) **Every event is written twice** — verified on five sampled days, the raw row count was exactly twice the distinct-row count and the multiplicity set was exactly {2}, so a naive notional sum *doubles* the statistic. The same hazard class appears independently in the Hyperliquid fill log, where Garcia Seuma (arXiv:2608.03616) records that "the liquidation object rides both legs of each trade, so all totals are deduplicated by the liquidated-user leg or they double-count": two venues, one hazard. (ii) **The quantity is in contracts, not in the asset**, and the COIN-M contract multiplier is 100 USD per contract (Binance's own specification), so notional is `quantity x 100` and **not** `quantity x price`; on 2024-08-05 the intuitive product gives **$17.06bn** of forced selling for BTCUSD_PERP where the correct figure is **$32.3m**, a **528-fold** overstatement that would have made the tail statistic look enormously powerful. (iii) **`side` is the order side**, so a SELL row is a liquidated **long** and a BUY row a liquidated short; the registered statistic counts SELL rows and must halve them for (i).

3. **The USDⓈ-M gap is now a determination, not an inference, and the catalogue's recorded fact is superseded in place.** Every prefix in the bucket was enumerated: `data/futures/um/daily/` holds aggTrades, bookDepth, bookTicker, indexPriceKlines, klines, markPriceKlines, metrics, premiumIndexKlines and trades and nothing else; `data/futures/cm/daily/` alone adds **liquidationSnapshot**; `data/option/daily/` holds only BVOLIndex and EOHSummary; `data/spot/daily/` only aggTrades, klines and trades. **There is no liquidation dataset anywhere in the USDⓈ-M tree** — and USDⓈ-M is the instrument CRYPTO-EDGE-001 specifies. The recorded fact "there is no liquidation dataset anywhere in the archive" was true of the UM tree and false of the CM tree, and is now narrowed rather than deleted.

4. **The registered tail statistic of CRYPTO-EDGE-001 is now computable on free data, in the venue's own contract unit, and the mandated proxy-fidelity step is now executable — and its first measurement is adverse to the proxy.** The COIN-M `metrics` tree carries the 5-minute `sum_open_interest` in **contracts** and, marker-paged, runs to **2026-10-19**, so it *does* overlap the liquidation window; the statistic "sell liquidation contracts / open interest contracts" is dimensionless and price-invariant by construction. The first same-unit measurement, on 2024-08-05 (a single-day construction description, not a performance statistic): BTCUSD_PERP recorded 322,865 SELL contracts = **1.96% of the opening open interest** against a **-13.6%** open-interest reduction, i.e. the forced flow accounts for **14.4%** of the state change; ETHUSD_PERP recorded 2,147,567 SELL contracts = **2.35%** against **-23.3%**, i.e. **10.1%**. The statistic separates the cascade day from the two adjacent days by roughly 4-5x (0.482% and 0.247% for BTC), and **the open-interest-clearing proxy registers a state change 10-14x larger than the forced flow that accounts for it** — so the proxy is a direction-and-severity indicator, not a measure of the flow, and the ratio must be reported by any validator.

5. **The free order-book depth tree was found, and it makes SaR computable on Binance.** `data/futures/{um,cm}/daily/bookDepth/{SYMBOL}/` runs **2023-01-01 to 2026-10-19** for both families, schema `timestamp,percentage,depth,notional`: a ten-level ladder at -5%..-1% and +1%..+5% of the mid, each level carrying depth and USD notional, at roughly 30-second sampling (2,880 timestamps per day). SaR (arXiv:2603.09164) requires exactly this kind of current-book microstructure and was demonstrated on a venue whose data is not free; the free Binance substitute reaches the core metrics (SaR, ESaR, TSaR) but **not** the framework's maker-concentration adjustment, because a bucketed ladder carries no maker identity. `data/futures/um/daily/bookTicker/` covers 2023-05-16 to 2024-03-30.

## Previous cycle (eleventh research cycle — the twelfth cycle was review-only and wrote no artefacts; its record is in HANDOFF.md)

A **correction cycle**. No hypothesis was added and no score moved; what the cycle produced is **four corrections to the catalogue's own recorded facts** — all of them *silent-failure* defects, which is the dangerous kind — and **two access determinations that change what a validator can actually do**. The tenth cycle's specifications were checked against the constructions they describe, and two of the three active entries' constructions were found to be wrong in ways that would have produced false answers rather than errors.

1. **The catalogue's recorded archive-pagination rule is wrong, and following it would have produced false terminal dates on essentially every symbol.** The recorded access note says the S3 listing endpoint "paginates with `<NextContinuationMarker>` rather than `<NextMarker>`". Inspected live on 2026-10-20, that is not what the endpoint does: with **no** delimiter the response contains **no continuation element at all** (only `<Marker>`, which merely echoes the request) and is truncated; with **a** delimiter it carries a standard `<NextMarker>`. The working method is therefore *no delimiter, and page by supplying `marker=<the last key returned>`* — verified end-to-end. A validator following the recorded rule finds no continuation token, concludes the listing is complete, and silently takes the **first** 1000 keys, whose last entry is the symbol's **500th** daily file. Measured on 20 sampled symbols, **20 of 20 were truncated**, and the naive "terminal date" is a mid-life date (BTCUSDT 2021-05-13, ETHUSDT 2021-05-13, SOLUSDT 2022-01-26, DOGEUSDT 2021-11-21, TONUSDT 2025-07-13, SUIUSDT 2024-09-13, and fourteen more). For CRYPTO-EDGE-004's point-in-time universe this is fatal in the *opposite* direction to the one the entry was reinstated to fix: still-trading contracts would be treated as delisted, at a mid-sample date, corrupting membership and momentum ranking together.

2. **The tenth cycle's terminal-state taxonomy is refuted, its rule is validated, and the correction was itself a correction.** The tenth cycle had amended "a delisted contract's series ends" versus "an inactive contract's series continues". Direct reads on 2026-10-20 show a **delisted** contract's series *continuing* with zero-volume, zero-trade files and a frozen close (IPUSDT, IPUSDC, PROMPTUSDT, 1000000BOBUSDT, RVVUSDT, YALAUSDT — all six continue), while the tenth cycle's LUNAUSDT case was delisted and *ended*. The terminal shape is a property of the venue's file emission and is **unrelated to delisting status**. The **rule** — last trading date = the last date with a non-zero trade count — survives, and is now **validated against an independent reference**: the venue's own dated delisting notices give the exact settlement datetime, and in all six instances checked the archive's last non-zero-trade date equals the announced settlement date exactly.

3. **The venue announcement feed is CONFIRMED: free, dated, machine-readable, and multi-year. This reverses the tenth cycle's "unconfirmed from this environment" determination and closes the feed sub-question of the deferred delisting family.** The CMS article catalog endpoint (`.../cms/article/catalog/list/query?catalogId=161&pageNo=N&pageSize=20`, HTTP 200, no key) plus the article detail endpoint (`.../article/detail/query?articleCode=<code>`, which returns `publishDate` as epoch milliseconds, the catalog name and id, and the body) yield **442 delisting articles across 23 pages back to 2022-02-24, of which 76 are perpetual-contract notices**, each giving the exact settlement datetime and the exact contract symbols. The public HTML detail pages carry the same content with a human-readable publication line, so the tenth cycle's record that the announcement pages sit behind a WAF captcha gate is true only of the *listing* page, not the detail pages or the CMS API. Bybit, Coinbase and BitMEX publish the same class of notice with the same mechanics, and **the venues' own settlement rules are now known**: Binance suspends the Futures Insurance Fund in the final hour and executes any resulting liquidation as a single IOCO offloaded into the market, with any unfilled portion resolved through auto-deleveraging; Bybit settles on the average index price of the **30 minutes** before delisting and Coinbase on the **60 minutes** before. The tenth cycle's own labelling of the failed access check as an *environment limitation* rather than a determination was the right call and is vindicated by the check succeeding later from a different route.

4. **The tenth cycle's binding question for CRYPTO-EDGE-001 is substantially answered, and the answer is partly adverse to the catalogue's own specification.** (i) The free open-interest-clearing proxy is a **state**; the payoff is a **flow**; and Garcia Seuma says so in print — "for the intraday analysis we necessarily observe the consequences of forced liquidation (open interest, aggressor flow) rather than liquidations themselves". The one window where both are published is the October 2025 cascade: open interest cleared 25-70% (to 0.30-0.55 of baseline on Hyperliquid, -24.6% intraday on Binance BTC) while $0.96bn of book-hitting forced sales crossed eighty 0.25%-wide price buckets, $733m of them after onset. (ii) **The v1.5 proxy had a units defect that would have produced a false positive**: it was specified on `sum_open_interest_value`, the **USD-notional** series, which moves with price, so a price decline with an unchanged contract count registers as a "clearing" containing zero liquidations. The same free metrics file carries `sum_open_interest`, the **contract-count** series, verified on 2026-10-20 (BTCUSDT, 2026-10-19 00:55 UTC: 93855.955 contracts, $7,721,663,336.37 notional). The proxy is now re-specified on the contract-count series. (iii) **The proxy's fidelity is now testable rather than arguable**, because two free forced-liquidation records exist: the **Hyperliquid on-chain forced-fill record** (per contract, per fill, with direction, 2025-03-27 to 2026-06-30, used by the AFT 2026 paper) and the **Zenodo daily eleven-venue panel** (365 days, $176.6bn, CC-BY-4.0, downloaded and inspected on 2026-10-20). The tenth cycle's statement that the published "sell liquidations as a share of open interest" statistic is "not computable on free data" is **too strong and is narrowed**: it is not computable from the Binance archive, and it *is* computable on free data on Hyperliquid and at daily cross-venue granularity.

5. **The state-variable question is now answered in a specific way, and the answer is adverse to CRYPTO-EDGE-004's framing without moving its score.** The **dispersion** variable — the entry's variable — has **no independent published test at all**: its only source is the entry's own working paper, plus two killed preregistered dial tests. A third, the **market-state transition** (Finance Research Letters 2025), competes as well. The honest reading is that CRYPTO-EDGE-004's claim is more likely **not the operative variable** than **wrong** — the framing the tenth cycle adopted, now with evidence.

6. **Two new sources bear on the *base* effect that CRYPTO-EDGE-004 conditions, one favourable and one adverse, and the score is still held.** arXiv:2510.14435v4 shows crypto size, momentum and value smart beta robust on a **survivorship-aware** universe of 18,622 live plus 29,230 delisted coins with historical data, which is directly adverse to the catalogue's survivorship-dependence objection. ## Statement
No empirical strategy validation (backtests, paper trading, forward tests, parameter sweeps) was performed in this repository. This is research documentation only. Published and preregistered results from external sources are cited as evidence about the world, not as results of any test run here. The seventh, eighth and ninth cycles read, fetched and checked external sources directly, including public API endpoints and public data archives whose availability, coverage and access limits were verified by hand; no return, Sharpe, drawdown or performance figure for any strategy in this catalogue was computed, and no strategy was executed. In the ninth cycle the only files fetched from the Binance public data archive were directory listings and a small number of single-day kline and metrics files, and they were read **solely** to determine data availability, listing and last-trading dates, and field definitions; no return, spread, price statistic or strategy result was computed from them.
