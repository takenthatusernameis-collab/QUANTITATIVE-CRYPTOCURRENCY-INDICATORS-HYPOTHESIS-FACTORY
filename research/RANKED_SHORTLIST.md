2. **CRYPTO-EDGE-001 is downgraded to 61/100, confidence low.** Two new findings bite, and they point in opposite directions. The published carry-crash paper's *own* crash-risk section is mined for the first time in this catalogue, and it directly supports the tail form of the prediction on dated futures: carry is strongly negatively related to implied and to realized skewness, a rise in carry of 10% predicts a 44% increase in total sell liquidations relative to total open interest over the next month, and carry predicts sell but not long liquidations. More damagingly, the *same authors'* earlier working paper measures carry as the funding rate of BTC/USD perpetual swaps and reports that it *positively* predicts returns in the BTC time series and in a cross-section of 51 cryptocurrencies. The published crash coefficient is a dated-futures coefficient; the entry's target instrument is the perpetual, and on the perpetual the original authors' own earlier result has the opposite mean sign. Added to that: four independent perpetual-era tests of funding and basis extremes find no unconditional edge and document cross-asset sign heterogeneity (BTC negative, ETH/BNB/XRP/DOGE positive, on a 789-day, three-venue, six-asset sample); the registered absolute trigger is unattainable on perps in the current regime (30-day basis about 5.2% annualized against a 4.5% T-bill rate, and the three-month basis below the two-year Treasury for 157 consecutive days since February 2026); and the 2025 cascade evidence runs the mechanism in the *opposite* direction, through basis compression and carry-trade unwinding rather than an elevated basis level. Evidence quality 4/5 -> 3/5, robustness 2/5 -> 1/5.

3. **CRYPTO-EDGE-003 is downgraded to 55/100, and the change is to the construction rather than to the claim.** The dispersion *level* has a documented half-life of 1.5 to 3.5 hours in a two-year, four-venue study, and a separate 26-exchange microstructure study finds intraday spread autocorrelations above 0.96 (ETH reverting in about 20 minutes) with spreads peaking about two hours after each settlement; a trailing-decile threshold on a quantity that reverts within hours is close to trading noise, so the signal must be re-registered on a slow clock. Against that, the *price-discovery objection* that dominated the last cycle's downgrade is now documented to be a poor general rule: the same 2026 study finds Bybit Granger-causing Binance funding rates in 62% of rolling windows against 21% in the reverse direction, and OKX 55% against 25%, so information discovery in funding rates does not follow a size hierarchy. Net value after frictions 2/5 -> 1/5, because the only direct cost test of the entry's registered construction finds that a threshold rule earns the largest gross income of any rule tested and finishes at -9% to -15% annualized after costs, while the construction that clears costs is a slow patient rebalance that does not use extreme-dispersion thresholds at all.

4. **CRYPTO-EDGE-004 is deferred at 45/100 and moves off the active shortlist.** The catalogue's own open question - is a survivorship-aware point-in-time crypto universe constructible from free data? - is now answered, and the answer is partly no. The CoinGecko free tier rejects every historical query (HTTP 401, error_code 10012, "Public API users are limited to querying historical data within the past 365 days"), so no free historical market-cap or market-cap-rank series back to 2019 is available there; CoinLore's free API is current-only. A listing-date and trailing-volume screen *is* constructible on free data from the Binance public archive, and a preregistered audit of exactly that construction retained 58% of Sharpe (1.47 to 0.86) and 49% of CAGR (+42.91% to +21.06%) for an unchanged cross-sectional momentum book, cleared neither a Sharpe of 1.0 nor a deflated-Sharpe bar of 0.95, and reversed its best year. The *delisting* half of survivorship is not observable on free data at all, and high cross-sectional dispersion - the entry's own test regime - is precisely where launches and delistings concentrate. Worse for the claim: the closest available preregistered test of the mechanism, a dispersion dial conditioning the same momentum book on cross-sectional dispersion, was **killed**, with the spread monotone *increasing* across dispersion terciles (+0.50%, +1.05%, +1.40%) and a top-minus-bottom difference of +0.90% with a 95% interval of [-2.66%, +3.69%] - the opposite direction to the entry's registered prediction - and a breadth-dial sibling was killed on the same pattern. Deferred, not rejected: the entry's registered prediction has not been tested and failed, and the falsification-handling rule is therefore not engaged.

5. **A second non-derivatives family was found, and deferred on a data-access ground rather than on evidence.** Token dilution and supply-schedule premia have a strong, recent, published result: 404 coins, weekly, 2020-2026, with the fully diluted valuation premium and a 12-week dilution rate predicting cross-sectional returns at Newey-West t-statistics of -3.13 and -2.79, portfolio sorts yielding 25-32% annualized long-short spreads, the premium absent in blue chips and mature coins and concentrated in the first year of trading, and a mechanism of *limited investor attention to disclosed vesting schedules* rather than mechanical price pressure. It is blocked by the same CoinGecko free-tier history restriction, because the signal needs dated circulating supply, fully diluted valuation and vesting schedules, and no free source of historical circulating supply was confirmed. The reinstatement condition - a free, dated, point-in-time supply or delisting record - is the same condition that would unblock or retire CRYPTO-EDGE-004, so the two families now share one decisive data question.

6. **Standing prior recorded for the next cycle, and it is sharper than last cycle's.** The carry and crowding family in crypto is *instrument-dependent*, not simply "continuation beats exhaustion". On dated futures, high carry predicts crashes (peer-reviewed, sample to July 2024). On perpetual funding, carry positively predicts returns (the same authors, 2021 working paper). On retail perps in 2024-2026, both the unconditional contrarian and the unconditional continuation forms fail, and the sign of any effect depends on the direction of the market on the event day and on the asset. Any new candidate in this domain must specify its instrument, its asset class and its conditioning state before it can be assessed, and **no candidate in this family may be promoted on the dated-futures evidence alone**.

One hypothesis rejected, two downgraded, one new non-derivatives entry. The cycle's dominant finding is a sign reversal that reorganises the catalogue.
1. **A preregistered external test of the funding-extreme contrarian thesis failed with the wrong sign**, so CRYPTO-EDGE-002 is rejected rather than downgraded. The test (Binance USDT perpetuals, 8 symbols, 17,910 symbol-days, December 2019 to July 2026) used this catalogue's own signal construction — trailing-90-day percentile of daily funding, 10th/90th deciles, fixed before the run — and found 7-day LOW-minus-HIGH of -0.95% with a 95% block-bootstrap interval of [-3.20%, +1.39%] and sign consistency of 4 of 8 symbols against a required 5. Both point-estimate directions leaned the wrong way: HIGH-funding days were followed by higher returns (+3.30% vs +2.35% at 7 days; +12.61% vs +4.83% at 30 days). A momentum-flavored variant was also tested and not promoted, so both signs are closed. This closes the catalogue's largest evidence gap for CRYPTO-EDGE-002, which until now rested only on practitioner anecdote.
2. **The same reversal hits CRYPTO-EDGE-001.** A preregistered test of the perp-basis level on Binance perpetuals (17,899 symbol-days) found the 7-day LOW-minus-HIGH at -2.61% with an interval of [-4.88%, -0.12%] excluding zero *on the wrong side*: high-premium days were followed by +4.20% vs +1.59% at 7 days and +14.8% vs +6.1% at 30 days. This is the closest available test of the entry's directional claim, it covers the post-July-2024 window that no published sample covers, and it contradicts the mean-return form of the prediction. CRYPTO-EDGE-001 is therefore restructured from a directional return claim into a **crash-conditionality** claim, with a mandatory volatility-matched baseline, and is downgraded from 73 to 63.
3. **CRYPTO-EDGE-003 is downgraded from 61 to 58** because its sign is now contested rather than merely uncertain: three independent sources place price discovery on the more heavily traded and typically higher-funding venue, and a preregistered cross-venue test found the high-premium venue was followed by *higher* returns (+3.17% at 7 days, CI [+0.51%, +6.08%], breadth 17/20, surviving a trailing-return trim and a concentration check). The same test found cross-venue dispersion *as a magnitude* to be noise at all three horizons. CRYPTO-EDGE-003's validation plan is reordered so the sign is estimated before any trade expression is specified.
4. **CRYPTO-EDGE-004 added at rank 3, 56/100, confidence low.** The catalogue's first non-derivatives entry: cross-sectional dispersion of crypto returns as a state variable that switches off cross-sectional momentum when the cross-section disagrees most. Published, methodologically explicit prior art (Zhang and Makgolo, April 2026); the blocking constraint is a free point-in-time crypto universe, not the idea.
5. **Standing prior recorded for the next cycle:** in crypto, crowding-style signals (funding extremes, elevated basis, cross-venue premiums) have now been contradicted in three preregistered tests, all in the same direction — they accompany continuation rather than exhaustion. Any new contrarian candidate in this domain must beat that prior before it is promoted.
