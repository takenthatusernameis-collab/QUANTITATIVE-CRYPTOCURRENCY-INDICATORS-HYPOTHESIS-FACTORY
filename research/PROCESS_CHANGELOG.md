- 2026-10-20: Process refinement — an explicit **evidence tier** was added for sources that are neither peer-reviewed studies nor practitioner anecdotes, and a **falsification-handling rule** was adopted. (i) Evidence tier: public, dated, preregistered research ledgers (a hypothesis document that fixes the signal, the data source, the thresholds and the pass bars before the test runs, and publishes the verdict and the trial count) are classified as a distinct tier between peer-reviewed evidence and practitioner material. They are ranked above practitioner sources because the prediction, the sample and the thresholds are auditable and the verdict is dated and irreversible; they are ranked below peer-reviewed work because they are unreviewed, unreplicated, self-published, and their own authors disclaim profitability. Every catalogue entry citing such a source must state the sample, the window and the non-peer-reviewed status, and must not describe it as a replication. (ii) Falsification-handling rule: when a hypothesis's *own registered prediction* has been directly tested and failed, the entry is downgraded or rejected on the evidence dimension and is not re-specified to dodge the result — the CRYPTO-EDGE-001 case shows the permitted move (restructuring the prediction into a narrower, better-identified claim that the failed test did not address, with the failed claim explicitly withdrawn and the new claim given its own falsification criteria) and the forbidden move (re-labelling the same return claim with a different threshold, window or venue). The rule exists because the most likely way a hypothesis catalogue rots is a sequence of well-meant re-specifications after each negative result, which converts a research queue into a list of unfalsifiable stories. (iii) A standing prior is recorded for the next cycles: in crypto, crowding-style signals have now been contradicted in three separate preregistered tests, all pointing the same way — they accompany continuation rather than exhaustion — so any new contrarian candidate in this domain must be argued against that prior before it can be promoted.

- 2026-10-20: Process refinement — the standing prior is **sharpened from a family-level rule to an instrument-level rule**, and a new instruction is added to the entry template. The sixth cycle recorded that in crypto, crowding-style signals have been contradicted in three preregistered tests, all pointing the same way: they accompany continuation rather than exhaustion. The seventh cycle found that this is too coarse to act on, because the *sign of the carry signal is instrument-dependent*: on dated futures, high carry predicts crashes (peer-reviewed, sample to July 2024), while on perpetual funding rates carry positively predicts returns (the same authors, 2021 working paper), and on retail perps in 2024-2026 both the unconditional contrarian and the unconditional continuation forms fail, with the sign of any effect depending on the direction of the market on the event day and on the asset. The refined prior is therefore: **no candidate in the funding, basis or crowding family may be promoted on dated-futures evidence, and every candidate must state its instrument, its asset class and its conditioning state before it can be assessed.** Reason: the sixth cycle's rule would have rejected a perp candidate for failing to beat a *futures* prior, which is the wrong comparison, and would have accepted a perp candidate whose sign was contradicted by the same authors on the perp itself; a rule that names the instrument cannot be satisfied by the wrong evidence. Expected benefit: candidates are compared against evidence drawn from the same market structure they claim to trade, which is the comparison that actually discriminates.

- 2026-10-20: Process refinement — **a free-data determination must be a construction, not a catalogue of endpoints, and a determination that concludes "the data does not exist" must be stated as a search over derivations as well as over fields.** The eighth cycle searched for a *field* named a dated delisting record and found none, and concluded that the delisting half of survivorship was unobservable on free data; the ninth cycle found the record inside a free price-and-volume archive, encoded not as a field but as the *absence of trades* in the terminal daily kline file of a contract that has stopped trading, and validated the construction against the venue's own announcements. The catalogue's own seventh-cycle source notes had already flagged that the Binance archive "supplies delisted-symbol prices" — the gap was not in the data but in the question that was asked of it. Reason: an archive is not a table of records; it is a set of dated observations, and the information a research question needs may be a property of the *series* rather than a value in a field. A determination that only enumerates fields will report "no" for inputs that are in fact derivable, and a false "no" is worse than an open question because it closes a research queue. Expected benefit: future cycles will look for constructions — derived quantities, event reconstructions, terminations, absences — before concluding that a data question is settled, and the third non-derivatives family search becomes a search over what can be *built*, not only over what can be *downloaded*.

- 2026-10-20: Process refinement — **a recorded access fact is a defect until it has been re-verified against the live response shape, because the failure mode of a wrong access note is silent truncation, not an error.** The catalogue's note that the Binance S3 listing endpoint "paginates with `<NextContinuationMarker>` rather than `<NextMarker>`" was wrong: with no delimiter the response carries **no** continuation element at all (only `<Marker>`, which echoes the request) and is truncated at 1000 keys, and the working method is to supply `marker=<the last key returned>`; with a delimiter the response does carry a standard `<NextMarker>`.
