- 2026-10-20: Process refinement — an explicit **evidence tier** was added for sources that are neither peer-reviewed studies nor practitioner anecdotes, and a **falsification-handling rule** was adopted. (i) Evidence tier: public, dated, preregistered research ledgers (a hypothesis document that fixes the signal, the data source, the thresholds and the pass bars before the test runs, and publishes the verdict and the trial count) are classified as a distinct tier between peer-reviewed evidence and practitioner material. They are ranked above practitioner sources because the prediction, the sample and the thresholds are auditable and the verdict is dated and irreversible; they are ranked below peer-reviewed work because they are unreviewed, unreplicated, self-published, and their own authors disclaim profitability. Every catalogue entry citing such a source must state the sample, the window and the non-peer-reviewed status, and must not describe it as a replication. (ii) Falsification-handling rule: when a hypothesis's *own registered prediction* has been directly tested and failed, the entry is downgraded or rejected on the evidence dimension and is not re-specified to dodge the result — the CRYPTO-EDGE-001 case shows the permitted move (restructuring the prediction into a narrower, better-identified claim that the failed test did not address, with the failed claim explicitly withdrawn and the new claim given its own falsification criteria) and the forbidden move (re-labelling the same return claim with a different threshold, window or venue). The rule exists because the most likely way a hypothesis catalogue rots is a sequence of well-meant re-specifications after each negative result, which converts a research queue into a list of unfalsifiable stories. (iii) A standing prior is recorded for the next cycles: in crypto, crowding-style signals have now been contradicted in three separate preregistered tests, all pointing the same way — they accompany continuation rather than exhaustion — so any new contrarian candidate in this domain must be argued against that prior before it can be promoted.

- 2026-10-20: Seventh research cycle completed. The cycle's three open questions from cycle 6 were answered, and two of the three answers were adverse. (a) The crash-conditionality of elevated basis on retail perpetuals after July 2024: the published carry-crash paper's own crash-risk section (BIS WP 1087 section 3.4) was mined for the first time in this catalogue and it *supports* the tail form of CRYPTO-EDGE-001's prediction on dated futures (carry strongly negatively related to implied and realized skew; a 10% rise in carry predicting a 44% increase in total sell liquidations relative to total open interest over the next month; carry predicting sell but not long liquidations) — so the sixth cycle's v1.2 restructure restored a published claim while discarding the published test of it. But the *same authors'* earlier working paper measures carry as the BTC/USD perpetual funding rate and reports that it *positively* predicts returns in the BTC time series and across 51 cryptocurrencies, which is an author-side, instrument-specific sign split on the exact instrument the entry targets; and four independent perpetual-era external tests (a conditional funding-arbitrage-capacity study, a funding-drag study, a cross-venue percentile study and a cross-asset funding-extreme study) find no unconditional edge and document cross-asset sign heterogeneity. CRYPTO-EDGE-001 was therefore downgraded 67 -> 61 (the 67 is the arithmetically correct v1.2 total) and confidence lowered to low; a separate registered arm was added for the basis-compression state, an open-interest-change baseline was made mandatory, and the unattainable absolute trigger was withdrawn as a parameter correction. - 2026-10-20: Process refinement — the standing prior is **sharpened from a family-level rule to an instrument-level rule**, and a new instruction is added to the entry template. The sixth cycle recorded that in crypto, crowding-style signals have been contradicted in three preregistered tests, all pointing the same way: they accompany continuation rather than exhaustion. The seventh cycle found that this is too coarse to act on, because the *sign of the carry signal is instrument-dependent*: on dated futures, high carry predicts crashes (peer-reviewed, sample to July 2024), while on perpetual funding rates carry positively predicts returns (the same authors, 2021 working paper), and on retail perps in 2024-2026 both the unconditional contrarian and the unconditional continuation forms fail, with the sign of any effect depending on the direction of the market on the event day and on the asset. The refined prior is therefore: **no candidate in the funding, basis or crowding family may be promoted on dated-futures evidence, and every candidate must state its instrument, its asset class and its conditioning state before it can be assessed.** Reason: the sixth cycle's rule would have rejected a perp candidate for failing to beat a *futures* prior, which is the wrong comparison, and would have accepted a perp candidate whose sign was contradicted by the same authors on the perp itself; a rule that names the instrument cannot be satisfied by the wrong evidence. Expected benefit: candidates are compared against evidence drawn from the same market structure they claim to trade, which is the comparison that actually discriminates.
- 2026-10-20: Process refinement — **a "free-data determination" is now a required output for any entry whose essential input is a dataset rather than a price series, and a "data question" may be recorded as the entry's next research question when it is binary, has no performance component, and gates the entry's testability.** The seventh cycle answered the catalogue's standing open question about point-in-time crypto universe constructibility by fetching the candidate endpoints directly rather than by reading about them, and the answer (CoinGecko free tier refuses historical queries; CoinLore is current-only; the Binance public archive supports a listing-date and volume screen; the delisting half is unfixable) was decisive enough to defer an entry outright. Reason: the previous cycles had recorded data availability as an open question for several cycles while the answer was a single API call away, and an entry whose essential input cannot be built on free data is not a research question but a documentation task. Expected benefit: the next cycle can convert a deferral into a rejection or a promotion in one step, and the catalogue stops carrying entries whose blocking constraint is already knowable.
