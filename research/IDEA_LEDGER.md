# IDEA LEDGER

This is the complete catalogue of research-only systematic cryptocurrency edge hypotheses. No empirical validation is performed in this repository.

Stable IDs: CRYPTO-EDGE-001, CRYPTO-EDGE-002 (rejected), CRYPTO-EDGE-003, CRYPTO-EDGE-004.

Scoring convention used in this file: for each rubric dimension, contribution = (score / 5) x dimension weight (weights in percentage points), so the seven contributions sum to a total out of 100. Cycle 1 records applied this formula incorrectly to the two 10%-weight dimensions (falsifiability and originality were each entered at half their proper value); all totals below use the stated formula. ## CRYPTO-EDGE-001 — Basis/Carry Reversal (Cash-and-Carry Crash Predictor), v1.2

1. Short name and version: Basis/Carry Reversal v1.2.
2. One-sentence thesis: Sustained elevated perpetual-futures basis (carry) relative to spot signals crowded leveraged longs and predicts elevated sell-liquidation risk and negative spot returns over the following weeks, because constrained arbitrage cannot immediately absorb the positioning imbalance.
3. Status: shortlisted (rank 1). Downgraded in the sixth research cycle from 73 to 63 and restructured from a directional return claim into a crash-conditionality claim; see items 6, 9, 13, 14, 15 and 16.
4. Proposed edge family and target market/universe: Derivatives pricing/positioning anomaly (cost-of-carry / constrained arbitrage); BTC and ETH USDT-margined perpetuals versus spot on major retail-accessible venues (Binance, Bybit, OKX); cross-venue where public data permits.
5. Mechanism: When spot demand outpaces futures-arbitrage capacity, perp basis widens positive. The published version of this account (Schmeling, Schrimpf & Todorov) attributes crypto carry to two forces: (i) demand from smaller, trend-chasing investors seeking leveraged upside exposure and (ii) limited deployment of arbitrage capital because of regulatory and margin frictions, notably the absence of cross-margining and venue leverage caps (Binance cut maximum leverage to 20x in July 2021). Cash-and-carry arbitrage is constrained by absent cross-margining, funding and custody costs, and short availability, so the positioning imbalance persists; carry traders are themselves exposed to liquidation if prices move against the trade before convergence. Chi (2023) documents the basis as the strongest cross-sectional crypto futures factor, consistent with spot-to-futures information segmentation. Counter-mechanism now recorded explicitly: He, Manela, Ross & von Wachter (2026) report that past returns explain the majority of the futures-spot gap in a time-series regression, which indicates that the basis level is at least partly a momentum / positive-feedback proxy rather than a pure positioning measure; carry and momentum are not separable in the raw signal. Structural amendment (v1.2): the *measurement* of this mechanism differs by instrument in a way that materially changes the trigger population. Ruan and Streltsov (CFTC comment letter, 21 May 2025) state that perpetual derivatives "trade with a much smaller basis to the spot market due to the funding rate mechanism, allowing for closer tracking of spot prices", and Ackerer, Hugonnier and Jermann (NBER w32936) report that 75% of Bitcoin futures daily volume and 94% of open interest in the first half of 2023 came from perpetual futures. The published carry-crash evidence is estimated on dated futures (OKEx, CME), whose basis is not mechanically suppressed by a funding rule; on retail perpetuals the same economic state produces a much smaller observable basis. A second amendment (v1.2): the informational content of perp funding appears to be about *volatility* rather than *direction*. A 2026 Journal of Financial Markets paper ("Perpetual futures as predictors of Bitcoin volatility", PII S0927539826000939) finds funding-rate-based predictors materially improve forecasts of Bitcoin spot volatility, with the predictive contribution remaining significant after controlling for fixed-maturity futures volatility measures, and interprets funding as capturing "imbalanced leveraged positions whose subsequent unwinding and the associated price fluctuations unfold over extended periods". This supports the positioning mechanism but relocates its predicted payoff from the sign of the forward return to the dispersion of the forward return.
6. Exact falsifiable prediction: **Restructured in v1.2 into a tail claim and a withdrawn mean claim, because a direct preregistered test of the v1.1 mean-return version failed with the opposite sign.** (P1, retained, primary) When annualized BTC/ETH perp basis exceeds a preregistered positive threshold (theory-derived starting value +20%) for a sustained period, the *frequency and severity of crashes* over the next 1-4 weeks are elevated — defined as the probability and the conditional magnitude of a forward drawdown exceeding a preregistered threshold — and sell liquidations as % of open interest rise; when basis is negative or near zero, no prediction is made. Direction: basis high -> higher left-tail risk. Horizon: 1-4 weeks. (P2, withdrawn as a directional prediction) The v1.1 statement that "spot returns over the next 1-4 weeks are negative on average" is withdrawn as a preregistered directional claim: a preregistered test on Binance USDT-margined perpetuals over December 2019 to July 2026 found that high-basis days were followed by *higher* forward returns (+4.20% versus +1.59% at 7 days; +14.8% versus +6.1% at 30 days), a difference whose 95% block-bootstrap interval excluded zero on the wrong side. It is retained only as a quantity to be re-estimated, not as a trade direction. (P3, new requirement) Because the mechanism is now modelled as a volatility/instability channel, the prediction must be evaluated as a *tail* statistic (crash probability and conditional crash severity, or the variance of forward returns), and both the tail statistic and the mean-return difference must be reported; success on the tail statistic with a wrong-signed mean difference is a pass on P1, and failure on P1 cannot be excused by a mean-return result in either direction. (P4) The v1.1 addendum stands: the same prediction must survive when the signal is the momentum-residualized basis (basis minus the part of the basis explained by trailing returns), otherwise the effect is not attributable to positioning.
7. Candidate explanatory variables and proposed signal concept: Signal = annualized perp basis (mark price minus spot index) / spot index, annualized using the funding/settlement cadence, computed at each 8-hour funding timestamp from only data known at that close (no look-ahead). Plain language: "is futures expensive relative to spot right now?" Forward window starts after the funding close at which the signal is observed.
8. Expected trade expression: Short perp (or reduce long exposure) when basis is extreme; optionally cash-and-carry unwind (long spot, short perp) where borrow and funding allow. Shorting is treated as a separate feasibility question (borrow cost, exchange rules). Not market-neutral by default; applied only in the basis-extreme regime.
9. Primary comparator or null explanation: Random-entry short with the same holding horizon and fee model; spot buy-and-hold over the same windows; basis computed on a venue-shuffled or lagged series as a placebo. High basis could also simply reflect rational cost of carry in a trending bull market, not crowded positioning. Mandatory added comparator (v1.1): a momentum-matched baseline (short when trailing returns are similarly elevated over the same window) and a momentum-residualized signal; if the momentum-matched baseline explains the effect, the positioning interpretation is not supported. Mandatory added comparator (v1.2): a *volatility-matched* baseline. Because perp funding has documented predictive content for forward spot volatility (Journal of Financial Markets, 2026), a validator must compare the high-basis state against states matched on realized volatility, not only on momentum. If high basis merely identifies high-volatility periods and the crash-risk conditionality vanishes once volatility is matched, then CRYPTO-EDGE-001 is a volatility-timing rule wearing a positioning label and the positioning interpretation is not supported. Two further mandatory controls (v1.2): a *carry-availability* control, because the cash-and-carry premium is documented to be effectively unpaid in the 2025-2026 regime (H62/H63 in the martex-quant ledger), so any test must report the trigger-state frequency separately in the pre- and post-2024 subsamples; and a *regime-split* control that reports the same statistics for the pre-July-2024 (published-sample) window and the post-July-2024 window, since the latter is the untested regime.
10. Data required, source URLs, free-access classification, granularity, likely history, coverage, limitations: perpetual mark price, spot index price, open interest, long/short liquidation notionals, funding rates; 8-hourly or daily; history at least 2 years. Sources for replication with free public endpoints (verified 2026-10-20): Binance GET https://fapi.binance.com/fapi/v1/fundingRate (public, no key, limit max 1000, includes fundingTime and markPrice; funding interval/cap/floor changes via GET /fapi/v1/fundingInfo); Bybit GET https://api.bybit.com/v5/market/funding/history (public, no key, 200 rows/page, USDT/USDC/inverse perps); OKX GET https://www.okx.com/api/v5/public/funding-rate-history (public, capped at 100 rows/call); Binance public data archive (https://data.binance.vision, documented in https://github.com/binance/binance-public-data) for klines/trades. Access classification: 1 (confirmed free, public) for funding, mark price and spot on major venues; 2 (apparently free, unverified depth/rate limits) for multi-year REST pagination depth, open interest, and liquidation notional detail. Note: the published carry-crash evidence (BIS WP 1087 / Management Science) used commercial vendor data (Skew, Coinmetrics); that is a source of the published result, not a requirement for a free replication.
11. Plausible friction and capacity considerations (theoretical, no measured values): funding paid on the short side erodes carry; short availability and borrow costs; slippage entering during stress; exchange leverage caps and liquidation of the arbitrage trade itself before convergence; turnover is limited to basis-extreme regimes so capacity is modest. No friction values are invented.
12. Why meaningfully different from the catalogue and prior art: Grounded in documented basis premium (Chi 2023) and carry-crash predictability (BIS WP 1087 / Management Science 2026), with a precise falsifiable liquidation-linked prediction and an explicit momentum-residualization requirement. Distinct from plain moving-average crossovers, from CRYPTO-EDGE-002 (level of cost of carry vs funding-flow extremes), and from CRYPTO-EDGE-003 (absolute time-series carry on a benchmark venue vs cross-venue relative dispersion).
13. Strongest argument against; likely failure regimes; confounders: (i) Basis can remain elevated through long trending bull markets (carry may be profitable while the trend continues). (ii) The published crash-predictive relation is estimated on dated futures (OKEx, CME) with data from Skew; transfer to perps on retail venues is untested, and Gornall, Rinaldi & Xiao (2025) report that perpetuals now dominate crypto derivatives volume and reduce extreme price dislocations, which mechanically shrinks the population of extreme-basis trigger states in the perp era. (iii) Crashes may be driven by exogenous news rather than positioning; Garcia Seuma (2026) finds that pre-crash signatures are event-heterogeneous and that the most attractive in-sample pattern from the October 2025 record cascade inverts out of sample on the August 2024 cascade, and that a pure news shock (February 2025 tariff episode) shows no precursor in any variable - a direct warning for any single-variable crash precursor. (iv) Liquidation data is noisy and often proxied. (v) Momentum explains much of the basis, so the raw signal may be a momentum proxy. (vi, new in v1.2 — the strongest single objection) A preregistered test on Binance USDT-margined perpetuals over December 2019 to July 2026 found that the *sign* of the high-basis state was backwards: the 7-day forward return difference between low-basis and high-basis states was -2.61% with a 95% block-bootstrap interval of [-4.88%, -0.12%], i.e. the interval excluded zero on the side opposite to the registered prediction, with high-premium days followed by +4.20% versus +1.59% at 7 days and +14.8% versus +6.1% at 30 days. This is the closest available test of CRYPTO-EDGE-001's directional claim, it covers the post-July-2024 window that the published evidence does not, and it contradicts the mean-return form of the prediction on the instrument family CRYPTO-EDGE-001 targets. It does not test crash conditionality, which is why v1.2 restructures the prediction rather than rejecting the entry outright. (vii, new in v1.2) The tradeable expression has little left to earn in the current regime: the cash-and-carry premium on the same venue complex is documented to be effectively unpaid over the most recent 365 days (+0.08%/yr always-on; +0.15%/yr with a funding filter), with the premium concentrated in earlier years. A crash-timing rule is then a low-frequency, high-variance overlay on a premium that is no longer being paid, and the short leg pays or receives funding in a regime where funding is near zero. Likely failure regimes: strong trending markets, exogenous shock events, regime change in venue leverage rules, and any regime in which perp basis is mechanically compressed by the funding mechanism.
14. Research-only validation plan to be executed elsewhere: preselect the basis threshold; define entry at funding close and exit at subsequent funding closes over 1-4 weeks; baseline versus random entry and versus momentum-matched entry; report both raw-basis and momentum-residualized signal variants; theoretical friction cost model (funding, spread, slippage, borrow); out-of-sample split with an explicit post-July-2024 subsample; regime coverage (bull, bear, sideways, liquidation-event windows); leave-one-venue-out check. **Amended in v1.2:** (a) the primary statistic is a *tail* statistic — the difference in the probability and conditional severity of a forward drawdown exceeding a preregistered threshold between high- and low-basis states — with the mean-return difference reported alongside it but not decisive; (b) the volatility-matched baseline from item 9 is mandatory and is computed on the same high/low state split; (c) the trigger-state *frequency* is reported separately for the pre- and post-July-2024 subsamples, because the perp-era basis distribution is mechanically compressed (Ruan and Streltsov 2025) and the carry premium is documented to be unpaid in 2025-2026, so a lower trigger rate and a lower trigger severity are different findings; (d) inference must be heavy-tail aware — Grobys and Shahzad (2026) show that the realised variances of crypto momentum strategies follow power laws and that the population mean and variance of the factor are statistically not defined under block bootstrap, so the validator should use block bootstrap on the statistic, report median-based summaries, and treat a Sharpe ratio as uninformative for the underlying factor; (e) falsification criteria: no elevated crash probability or crash severity after high basis, no rise in sell liquidations as % of OI, the effect vanishing once momentum is controlled, or the effect vanishing once realized volatility is matched. Do not execute this plan in this repository.

## CRYPTO-EDGE-002 — Funding Extreme Contrarian (Crowded Leverage Unwind), v1.2

1. Short name and version: Funding Extreme Contrarian v1.2.
2. One-sentence thesis: Extreme funding-rate readings (top/bottom decile of the trailing distribution) signal overcrowded leveraged positioning prone to forced unwind, predicting short-horizon mean reversion opposite to the funding extreme.
3. Status: **rejected** (was shortlisted at rank 3 until the sixth research cycle). Removed from the ranked shortlist; the reason is recorded in REJECTED_AND_DEFERRED.md so the idea is not rediscovered. Reinstated only if a material new fact appears, per item 17.
4. Proposed edge family and target market/universe: Derivatives positioning/flow anomaly (crowded leverage, forced liquidation feedback); BTC and ETH perpetuals on retail-accessible venues with public funding history.
5. Mechanism: Positive funding means longs pay shorts - crowded longs. When sentiment turns, leveraged longs hit maintenance margin; the exchange emits forced, price-insensitive market sells, pushing price further down into the next thresholds - the leverage-manufactured feedback documented in the liquidation-cascade literature (Garcia Seuma 2026; BitMEX study showing ~60X average leverage among the liquidated and substantial daily liquidation %). Crowding persists because momentum chasers enter late and leverage is sticky. Mirror image for extreme negative funding (crowded shorts, short-squeeze exposure). Structural amendment (v1.1): the funding rate is by construction proportional to the futures-spot gap, so a gap driven by momentum / positive feedback generates the same funding extremes as a gap driven by crowding; the mechanism therefore does not by itself distinguish this hypothesis from a momentum-reversal rule, and the perp-era compression of dislocations means extreme funding states are rarer and less extreme than in the pre-2022 sample. Structural amendment (v1.2): the market-structure description is retained, but the inference from crowding to imminent reversal is withdrawn, because three preregistered tests in a public ledger now show that in crypto a crowding-style extreme is followed by *continuation* rather than exhaustion (see items 6, 13 and 15) — funding extremes, elevated perp basis and cross-venue premiums all failed to reverse and all leaned the wrong way. The forced-liquidation feedback loop itself is unaffected and remains a published finding; what the evidence rejects is the timing claim that an extreme is followed by an unwind. This distinction is the reason the entry is rejected as a *return* hypothesis rather than as a *mechanism*.
6. Exact falsifiable prediction: Funding rate in the top decile of the trailing 90-day distribution predicts negative returns over the next 1-7 days; bottom decile predicts positive returns. Direction: opposite to funding sign. Horizon: 1-7 days. Addendum (v1.1): the prediction must hold incrementally after controlling for CRYPTO-EDGE-001 basis and for trailing returns; if the decile signal adds nothing beyond those, it is not a distinct edge. **Outcome (v1.2): the registered prediction was tested and failed on both preregistered criteria, with point estimates of the wrong sign.** The test used the same signal construction as this entry (trailing-90-day percentile rank of daily funding, LOW <= 10th, HIGH >= 90th, fixed in advance), on Binance USDT-margined perpetuals joined to spot, across 8 symbols and 17,910 symbol-days from December 2019 to July 2026 — a window that covers the post-July-2024 period this entry's own open question had identified as untested. The 7-day LOW-minus-HIGH difference was -0.95% with a 95% moving-block bootstrap CI of [-3.20%, +1.39%], and the sign was positive in only 4 of 8 symbols against a preregistered requirement of 5. Both point-estimate directions leaned the wrong way: HIGH-funding days were followed by +3.30% versus +2.35% at 7 days and +12.61% versus +4.83% at 30 days. A momentum-flavored variant (HIGH funding read as confirmation rather than exhaustion) was tested in the same ledger and also failed to clear its bar at 30 days (+7.78%, CI [-0.77%, +17.58%]), so the opposite sign was *not* promoted either. Both directions of the funding-extreme claim are therefore closed on the available evidence.
7. Candidate explanatory variables and proposed signal concept: Signal = 8-hour funding rate from public exchange data, expressed as a percentile of the trailing 90-day distribution computed using only past data (no look-ahead); signal observed at a funding timestamp, forward window starts after. Plain language: "are traders paying an extreme premium to hold one side?"
8. Expected trade expression: Contrarian - short when funding is extremely positive, long when extremely negative; shorting feasibility is a separate question (borrow, exchange constraints). Not market-neutral; avoid strong trending news regimes as a filter. Alignment note: when contrarian direction coincides with receiving funding, the carry helps rather than hurts.
9. Primary comparator or null explanation: Random entry with the same hold period; spot buy-and-hold; funding series time-shuffled as a placebo; CRYPTO-EDGE-001 (basis level) as an alternative positioning proxy to test whether funding adds incremental information; momentum-matched entry (v1.1, mandatory) given that the funding rate is proportional to the futures-spot gap.
10. Data required: 8-hour funding rates, mark price, spot price; open interest as context; history at least 2 years; free public endpoints verified 2026-10-20 - Binance /fapi/v1/fundingRate, Bybit /v5/market/funding/history, OKX /api/v5/public/funding-rate-history, Binance public data archive for klines. Access classification: 1 (confirmed free, public) for funding and price on major venues; 2 (granularity/history limits unverified) for open interest and liquidation detail.
11. Plausible friction and capacity considerations: funding receipt is favorable in the contrarian direction when sign-aligned, but adverse when it is not; slippage and whipsaws in strong trends can dominate; exchange-specific funding formula and clip; exchange leverage rules affect how quickly unwinds occur. No measured friction values.
12. Why meaningfully different from the catalogue and prior art: Mechanism is funding-rate flow extremes and forced-unwind feedback, distinct from the carry level in CRYPTO-EDGE-001 and from the cross-venue relative measure in CRYPTO-EDGE-003. Distinct from generic price oscillators (e.g. RSI overbought) because it uses derivatives positioning, not price history alone - but note (v1.1) that the informational content of the funding extreme overlaps materially with momentum.
13. Strongest argument against; likely failure regimes; confounders: (i) Extremes can persist for extended trending periods (funding can stay positive for months in a bull run). (ii) Timing of the unwind is unpredictable; false positives are frequent. (iii) Little rigorous published evidence ties funding percentiles specifically to forward returns - most sources are practitioner/anecdotal. (iv) He et al. (2026) show the futures-spot gap is largely explained by past returns, so a funding extreme is largely a momentum extreme; the contrarian rule may be a momentum-reversal rule wearing a derivatives label. (v) Zhivkov (2026) reports funding rates clustering near zero across venues (interquartile range 1.20 bps for centralized venues), so genuine extremes are rare events and any percentile threshold will be estimated on a thin tail. (vi) Gornall, Rinaldi & Xiao (2025) report that perpetuals reduce extreme price dislocations, which mechanically shrinks the trigger population in the perp era. (vii) Garcia Seuma (2026) shows liquidation timing is not predictable from a stable precursor, and that a news-driven cascade shows no precursor at all. (viii, added in v1.2 — decisive) The mechanism's central premise is empirically backwards in crypto: crowding does not mark exhaustion, it accompanies continuation. The preregistered test of this entry's registered prediction failed on both of its criteria with wrong-signed point estimates (item 6), and the same ledger's sibling test of the perp-basis level failed in the same direction (CRYPTO-EDGE-001, item 15), and its cross-venue continuation test also failed in the continuation direction. Three preregistered tests in one public ledger, covering funding extremes, perp basis levels and cross-venue premiums, all found that the crowding signal predicts *more* of the same rather than a reversal — a consistent pattern that is far more informative than any single failed test. The mechanism can still be true about *forced liquidation mechanics* (Garcia Seuma 2026; BitMEX 2021); what fails is the inference from crowding to imminent reversal. Likely failure regimes: strong momentum regimes, exogenous news shocks, and — for this entry specifically — every regime, since the sign of the point estimates was wrong rather than merely imprecise.
14. ## CRYPTO-EDGE-003 — Cross-Venue Funding Dispersion (Fragmentation Relative Value), v1.1

1. Short name and version: Cross-Venue Funding Dispersion v1.1.
2. One-sentence thesis: Extreme dispersion of perpetual funding rates across trading venues for the same asset identifies venue-specific positioning or liquidity imbalances that the integrated multi-venue market has not priced, so the venue with the anomalously high funding rate subsequently underperforms the cross-venue median perpetual on a 5-20 day horizon, with the funding differential persisting long enough to be partly harvestable.
3. Status: developing (shortlisted, rank 2). Downgraded in the sixth research cycle from 61 to 58; see items 13, 15 and 16 for the new contrary evidence and the required sign estimation before the trade expression can be specified.
4. Proposed edge family and target market/universe: Cross-venue market-fragmentation / relative-value derivatives (segmented arbitrage, venue-specific liquidity and counterparty premia); BTC and ETH USDT-margined perpetuals on Binance, Bybit and OKX (extendable to other venues where funding history and volume filters are available).
5. Mechanism: Perpetual funding rates are strongly but not perfectly correlated across venues because arbitrage across venues is segmented: exchanges do not offer cross-margining, so an arbitrageur exploiting a funding differential must fund and margin both legs separately, tying up capital on each venue and limiting the capital that can respond to any given discrepancy; venues also differ substantially in liquidity, and a venue-specific shock (withdrawal of market makers, collateral or stablecoin stress, venue-specific news) can produce a local imbalance. The literature documents that this residual dispersion persists: He, Manela, Ross & von Wachter (2026) report Binance-Bybit funding correlation of 0.74 with meaningful divergences during unusually large funding payments, and state explicitly that the residual cross-exchange dispersion reflects the limits of arbitrage across segmented venues and is a promising avenue for future research; Zhivkov (2026) finds that transaction costs and spread-reversal risks prevent arbitrage from eliminating large mispricings between platforms and that market frictions show up in cross-platform spreads rather than average levels, with persistent two-tier structure in which centralized venues lead price discovery. Two distinct stories can generate a high funding rate at one venue - locally crowded longs that will be force-unwound, or a locally scarce instrument whose market makers have withdrawn - and both imply the expensive venue's perpetual is biased rich relative to the integrated price; a third, contrary story is that the high-funding venue is the price-leading, information-rich venue, in which case the expensive venue should outperform. The hypothesis asserts the first two and is falsified by the third. **Amendment (v1.1): the third story is now the better-supported one, not the minority one.** Three independent sources place the price-discovery role on the more heavily traded, typically higher-funding venue rather than the thin one: Gornall, Rinaldi and Xiao (2025) report that perpetuals lead spot price discovery; Zhivkov (2026) finds that centralized venues lead price discovery with 61 percent higher information integration than decentralized venues and that information flow runs CEX to DEX with no reverse causality; and He, Manela, Ross and von Wachter (2026) attribute the residual cross-exchange funding dispersion to segmented arbitrage and liquidity differences. The mechanism that would make the *expensive* venue underperform therefore requires the thin venue to be the one with locally crowded longs, while the mechanism that makes the expensive venue *outperform* requires only that the expensive venue is the price leader — which is what the published evidence describes. A third, newly documented possibility (v1.1): the dispersion may be a *liquidity-withdrawal artifact* rather than a positioning imbalance at all, and the closest adjacent preregistered test of cross-venue disagreement as a magnitude found no predictive content whatsoever (see item 15).
6. Exact falsifiable prediction: Define d_v,t = (venue-v 8-hour funding rate for symbol s at funding timestamp t, normalized to a common 8-hour-equivalent basis) minus (the cross-venue median funding rate for the same symbol at the same timestamp). When d_v,t is in the top decile of its trailing 90-day distribution (and, in the mirrored case, the bottom decile), the venue-v perpetual's return over the next 5 to 20 days is below (above) the median return of the same symbol's perpetuals across the other venues, and the funding differential itself does not fully close within one to three funding intervals. Direction: high relative funding -> relative underperformance of the expensive venue. Horizon: 5-20 days for relative performance; 1-3 funding intervals for dispersion persistence. **Amendment (v1.1): the sign is now a parameter to be estimated, not an assumption to be tested.** The registered direction above is the contrarian reading. The weight of evidence has shifted toward the continuation reading (the expensive venue is the price leader and outperforms), so a validator must estimate the sign before trading it: the mandatory first step is a two-sided estimation of E[relative return of the expensive venue | extreme dispersion] with a block-bootstrap interval, and the trade direction must follow the estimated sign, not the registered one. If the estimated interval excludes zero on the continuation side, the contrarian trade in this entry is closed and the entry should be rewritten as a continuation hypothesis or rejected. The dispersion-persistence sub-claim (1-3 funding intervals) is unaffected by the sign question and should be tested independently, because it is the part of the hypothesis that the published microstructure evidence actually addresses.
7. Candidate explanatory variables and proposed signal concept: Signal = cross-venue funding dispersion d_v,t (a relative, venue-demeaned quantity), computed only from funding rates settled at or before timestamp t; venue pair selection and all thresholds from trailing distributions only; forward window starts after the funding settlement. Plain language: "is this one venue's perpetual unusually expensive relative to the same asset priced elsewhere?" Because the measure differences out the common component of carry - which He et al. show is largely momentum-driven and co-moving across currencies - it targets venue-specific segmentation rather than market-wide carry.
8. Expected trade expression: Market-neutral relative value: short the expensive venue's perpetual and long the cheap venue's perpetual (or hedge the short with spot on the same venue), equal notional, no directional beta; the funding received on the short leg offsets carry paid on the long leg when the differential is positive. Not a directional long/short market bet. Feasibility caveats: margin must be posted on both venues, collateral must exist on both, and venue or collateral risk (for example a stablecoin depeg or venue insolvency) breaks the hedge rather than being diversifiable.
9. Primary comparator or null explanation: (i) a random venue pair traded with the same notional and dates; (ii) the same trade restricted to low-dispersion dates; (iii) the liquidity-artifact null - the dispersion must survive filtering venues by volume and open interest and using mark prices rather than last trades; (iv) the momentum null - the signal must add value after residualizing against trailing returns and against CRYPTO-EDGE-001 basis; (v) the price-discovery null, in which the expensive venue leads and the trade should be reversed; (vi) a sign placebo using the mirrored dispersion signal to detect implementation errors.
10. Data required, source URLs, free-access classification, granularity, likely history, coverage, limitations: per-venue 8-hour funding history and per-venue perpetual mark prices and klines for the same symbol; per-venue volume/open interest for liquidity filters. Verified free public sources (checked 2026-10-20): Binance GET https://fapi.binance.com/fapi/v1/fundingRate (public, no key, limit max 1000, returns fundingRate, fundingTime, markPrice; funding interval/cap/floor adjustments via GET /fapi/v1/fundingInfo) - access 1 for endpoint availability, 2 for multi-year pagination depth; Bybit GET https://api.bybit.com/v5/market/funding/history (public, no key, 200 rows per page, covers USDT/USDC/inverse perps) - access 1; OKX GET https://www.okx.com/api/v5/public/funding-rate-history (public, capped at 100 rows per call) - access 1 for endpoint availability, 2 for retention depth; Binance public data archive https://data.binance.vision (documented at https://github.com/binance/binance-public-data) for monthly/daily klines and trades - access 1. (ii) The sign may be the opposite of the stated prediction if the high-funding venue is the price-discovery leader (Gornall, Rinaldi & Xiao 2025 report perpetuals lead spot price discovery; Ackerer, Hugonnier & Jermann document perpetual pricing and price discovery effects), in which case buying the expensive venue and shorting the cheap one is the profitable expression. **Promoted to the primary objection in v1.1:** this is no longer the minority reading. ## CRYPTO-EDGE-004 — Dispersion-Contingent Momentum Breakdown (Cross-Sectional Disagreement as a Momentum-Reliability Switch), v1.0

1. Short name and version: Dispersion-Contingent Momentum Breakdown v1.0.
2. One-sentence thesis: When the cross-section of crypto assets is unusually dispersed in its returns — that is, when coins disagree most sharply about direction — the cross-sectional ranking of trailing returns becomes dominated by asset-specific noise rather than by continuation, so the long-winners/short-losers momentum spread collapses and momentum exposure should be cut or reversed in that state.
3. Status: new (shortlisted, rank 3). Added in the sixth research cycle as the catalogue's first non-derivatives entry.
4. Proposed edge family and target market/universe: Cross-sectional breadth / market-structure regime variable applied to cross-sectional momentum; a survivorship-aware universe of liquid USDT-quoted crypto spot pairs (BTC, ETH and the top ~50-100 tokens by contemporaneous market capitalisation and quote volume) on major venues (Binance, OKX, Bybit, Coinbase).
5. Mechanism: cross-sectional dispersion measures disagreement about the direction of the common factor, and it acts on the *reliability* of a ranking signal through two channels. (a) *Signal-to-noise channel*: the momentum payoff is delivered largely through the common (market) trend component; when dispersion is low, tokens co-move, the cross-sectional ranking is driven by differences in exposure and beta, and the ranking is stable and informative; when dispersion is high, the cross-section is dominated by idiosyncratic, asset-specific news (token unlocks, listings, meme rotations, idiosyncratic liquidations, protocol events), which raises the variance of the ranking error faster than it raises expected continuation, so the momentum spread's signal-to-noise ratio falls — and because crypto factor payoffs have power-law tails, a fall in signal-to-noise can push the mean below estimability rather than merely shrink it. (b) *Crowding channel*: high-dispersion states are also the states in which momentum positions are most crowded and most exposed to a common-factor reversal, so the momentum spread's *left tail* is fattest exactly in the high-dispersion regime. Why it may persist: the state variable is slow-moving relative to the formation window and is not itself the traded signal, so it does not attract the arbitrage that would erode the base momentum effect; it is a risk-budgeting switch on a known effect rather than an alpha source in its own right. Competing explanation that must be tested rather than assumed: dispersion may be a proxy for a *market-wide volatility regime* that independently damages momentum; the published evidence claims the effect is not explained by generic market volatility or by average cross-asset correlation, but a free replication must redo that orthogonalisation.
6. Exact falsifiable prediction: let D_t be the cross-sectional dispersion of trailing 30-day returns across the point-in-time universe (for example the cross-sectional standard deviation, or the interquartile range, of trailing 30-day returns), computed from data known at t. Condition dates into deciles of D_t computed from trailing history only. Prediction: the winner-minus-loser cross-sectional momentum spread over the next 1 month (top decile of trailing 30-day return minus bottom decile, equal-weighted, same point-in-time universe) is significantly *smaller* in the top decile of D_t than in the bottom decile; the effect is nonlinear and concentrated in the upper tail of dispersion, so the middle deciles should show little difference; and the effect must remain after controlling for (i) Bitcoin realized volatility over the same window and (ii) the average pairwise cross-asset correlation across the universe. Falsification: the momentum spread is equal or larger in the high-dispersion regime; or the difference disappears once Bitcoin realized volatility and average cross-asset correlation are controlled; or the effect is present only in a single sub-period or only in one era.
7. Candidate explanatory variables and proposed signal concept: signal = a *state variable* (cross-sectional dispersion of trailing 30-day returns) used to switch or scale a momentum exposure, not itself a directional signal. Plain language: "when the coins are pulling in opposite directions, the winners-and-losers ranking has stopped working." Timing: D_t is computed from returns up to and including t; both the momentum ranking and the forward window start after t. Universe membership at t must be determined using only information available at t (listing age, market-cap and volume screens computed on trailing data only).
8. Expected trade expression: cross-sectional within the universe: long the top trailing-return decile and short the bottom, with exposure scaled by the dispersion state (full weight at low dispersion, zero or negative at high dispersion) — equivalently, the dispersion state as a filter on an otherwise unconditional momentum book. Shorting feasibility is a separate question (borrow availability, funding paid on the short leg, venue rules, margin). The expression is a risk-budgeting switch on a known base effect, not a standalone directional bet.
9. Primary comparator or null explanation: (i) unconditional cross-sectional momentum on the identical universe and dates — the mandatory incremental bar; (ii) the same momentum book scaled by a *Bitcoin-realized-volatility* regime variable instead of dispersion (the generic-market-state null); (iii) the same momentum book scaled by the average pairwise cross-asset correlation (the redundancy null); (iv) momentum conditioned on trailing market-cap or liquidity (a size/quality null); (v) a time-shuffled dispersion series as a placebo; (vi) a random-assignment placebo in which the dispersion state is permuted across dates, preserving its distribution but destroying its timing. Falsification criteria are controls (ii) and (iii) reproducing the effect, which would mean dispersion adds nothing beyond generic market state.
10. Data required: (a) point-in-time universe membership — listing date, contemporaneous market capitalisation, and quote volume for each token; (b) daily spot close and quote volume per token per venue. Free sources (checked 2026-10-20): CoinGecko public API `/api/v3/coins/markets` (keyless public tier with rate limits and a documented cache; documentation at https://docs.coingecko.com/reference/coins-markets — exposes market cap, market-cap rank, total volume, circulating supply, fully diluted valuation and price-change windows; **access 2** — the keyless tier returns the *current* universe and current market caps, so a point-in-time universe must be reconstructed from a historical snapshot source, and that reconstruction is not confirmed feasible on free data); Binance public data archive https://data.binance.vision (monthly and daily klines and trades for spot pairs, including symbols subsequently delisted — **access 1** for prices); OKX public candle history and Bybit V5 kline endpoints (**access 1** for prices). Known limitations: survivorship — a token delisted after a collapse must remain in the universe with mark-to-next-delisting treatment, otherwise the high-dispersion regime is precisely the regime in which the sample is thinned; free historical market-cap snapshots are the binding constraint; low-liquidity tokens have stale closes and inflated return variance; venue fragmentation means the universe must be defined per venue or on one benchmark venue with an explicit choice.
11. Plausible friction and capacity considerations: cross-sectional crypto trades pay taker fees on both legs, spread and slippage on the short leg, funding on the short leg of a perpetual implementation, and margin on two sides; with a monthly formation window turnover is not dominant, but the short leg's borrow and the tail risk of a crowded long book are the real costs; capacity in the smallest tokens is poor, so a liquidity screen is mandatory; because the base momentum effect's realised variance is heavy-tailed, drawdown control dominates any expected-value estimate. No friction values are invented.
12. Why meaningfully different from the catalogue and prior art: it is the catalogue's only non-derivatives entry and its only cross-sectional breadth mechanism. CRYPTO-EDGE-001, -002 and -003 are all single-instrument derivatives positioning measures acting on a market-wide factor; this entry measures *disagreement across assets* and acts on the *ranking* of assets rather than on the direction of the market. It is not a renamed moving-average crossover, not a single-asset volatility filter, and not another indicator added to a list. Prior-art scoping: the effect has already been published — Zhang and Makgolo (2026), "Cross-Sectional Dispersion and the State Dependence of Cryptocurrency Momentum" — which is why the originality score is held to 3/5 and why the entry is scoped as a *retail-testable, free-data, survivorship-aware specification* of a published state-dependence rather than as a new claim. The marginal contribution claimed here is (a) a preregisterable free-data test design for a published effect, and (b) the explicit heavy-tail inference requirement that the momentum-factor literature requires, which the published work does not address for a tradable specification. Novelty is not claimed globally, and the scope of the literature search is recorded in SOURCE_NOTES.md.
13. Strongest argument against; likely failure regimes; confounders: (i) **the base effect may not exist in a tradeable form** — Grobys and Sapkota (2019) find no momentum in crypto for 2014-2018, and Grobys and Shahzad (2026) show that the realised variances of six crypto momentum strategies follow power laws so that the population mean and variance are statistically not defined under block bootstrap, concluding that variance-based performance metrics are uninformative; a conditional overlay on a base effect whose mean may be unestimable is a weak proposition. (ii) **the incremental content may be nil** — the published claim is that dispersion adds information beyond Bitcoin realized volatility and average cross-asset correlation; if the crypto momentum spread is already dominated by generic market state, dispersion is a noisy proxy for it. (iii) **universe construction drives the answer** — the published work states that inference is sensitive to dynamic universe construction, and a free-data implementation is more exposed to survivorship than the original. (iv) **heavy tails cut both ways** — in the high-dispersion regime the momentum spread's left tail is fattest, so the mean can fall even where the median does not, which makes a mean-based test the wrong instrument and can make a real effect look like a null. (v) **confounding with launches and unlocks** — a high-dispersion state is often produced by a small number of new or newly issued tokens entering the universe, so the state variable may be a proxy for "the universe changed" rather than for disagreement. (vi) **crowding** — the momentum book is the most crowded cross-sectional book in crypto, so the high-dispersion regime is also when it is most likely to be unwound together, which is an argument for the overlay and a confound for its attribution. Likely failure regimes: low-volatility, high-momentum periods where the overlay would suppress the base effect; a universe whose membership churns; any window where the top-decile winners are driven by a single idiosyncratic token.
14. Research-only validation plan to be executed elsewhere: preregister the universe construction rule (listing-age, market-cap and volume screens computed on trailing data), the dispersion definition (cross-sectional standard deviation of trailing 30-day returns over the point-in-time universe), the momentum specification (trailing 30-day return ranking, top-minus-bottom decile, equal weight, 1-month forward window, non-overlapping), the state variable (trailing percentile of D_t) and all thresholds before any data is inspected. Data requirements: point-in-time universe plus daily closes for every member including delisted tokens, with a survivorship-aware backfill that marks a delisting at its last traded price and carries the position to a zero or a preregistered terminal value. Predeclared rules: the primary statistic is the difference in the forward momentum spread between the top and bottom decile of D_t; the incremental bar is that this difference must be larger than the unconditional momentum spread and must survive control for Bitcoin realized volatility and average cross-asset correlation. Inference: block bootstrap on dates with the cross-section kept intact within blocks, median-based summaries, and an explicit statement that a Sharpe ratio is not an informative summary for a power-law-tailed factor (Grobys and Shahzad 2026); report the *volatility of the signal bucket* alongside the mean difference. Out-of-sample design: time-based split with the dispersion threshold and the universe rule fixed in the training period; leave-one-asset-out and leave-one-year checks; a pre-2022 versus post-2022 split. Regime coverage: trending bull, bear, high-volatility, and token-launch-heavy windows. Cost model: taker fees and slippage on both legs, funding on the short leg of a perpetual implementation, borrow where spot is used. Falsification criteria: no difference in the forward momentum spread between dispersion states; the difference vanishing after volatility and correlation controls; the effect appearing only in one sub-period; the effect depending on the exclusion of the two or three largest contributors; or the momentum base effect itself being non-estimable, in which case the entry is rejected rather than refined. Do not execute this plan in this repository.

Evidence notes with sources and dates: Zhang, Cong and Makgolo, Olorato MacDonald, "Cross-Sectional Dispersion and the State Dependence of Cryptocurrency Momentum" (written 25 April 2026, last revised 27 April 2026), SSRN 6648082, abstract freely readable via the SSRN abstract page and search index; the SSRN PDF delivery URL returned HTTP 403 to automated fetch on 2026-10-20, so **access 2**. Provides: a dynamic, survivorship-aware universe reconstructed from the contemporaneous CoinGecko top 500 with rolling screens for age, market capitalisation, trading activity and return quality; the finding that momentum remains economically meaningful but that *lagged cross-sectional dispersion*, not generic market volatility, is the variable most closely associated with subsequent momentum breakdown, established in horse-race regressions, orthogonalization tests and asset-level panel specifications controlling for Bitcoin realized volatility and average cross-asset correlation; that the effect is nonlinear and concentrated in the upper tail of dispersion, consistent with deterioration in the reliability of the ranking signal; that dispersion-based scaling improves full-sample drawdown and certainty-equivalent outcomes while Bitcoin-volatility scaling performs better in post-2020 long-only implementation exercises; and that inference about cryptocurrency momentum is sensitive to dynamic universe construction. Establishes: a published, methodologically explicit state-dependence of crypto momentum on cross-sectional dispersion. Does not establish: a net-of-costs tradable payoff, a forward-return claim at the individual-token level, or an independent replication — the working paper is not peer-reviewed and no replication was found in this cycle's searches. Grobys, Klaus and Shahzad, Syed Jawad Hussain, "Cryptocurrency Momentum: Is It an Illusion?", International Journal of Finance and Economics 31(2):2180-2193 (April 2026), doi 10.1002/ijfe.70036, abstract freely readable via the publisher abstract page and the RePEc/IDEAS index, access 2; provides: the realised variances of six cryptocurrency momentum strategies follow power laws; the population mean and variance of the momentum factor realised variances are statistically not defined under block bootstrap; the performance metrics evaluating the profitability of crypto momentum strategies that use variance as an input are not informative; and there is cross-sectional dependence in the tail risk of momentum strategies built on different formation periods; establishes the inference constraint used in items 11, 13 and 14, and is the strongest published objection to any momentum-based entry; does not address dispersion conditioning. Grobys, Klaus, Kolari, James W., Sandretto, Davide, Shahzad and Aijo (2025), "Cryptocurrency momentum has (not) its moments", Financial Markets and Portfolio Management 39:443-476, free PDF at https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s11408-025-00474-9.pdf, access 1 for the PDF; provides evidence that crypto momentum performance is state-dependent and suffers poor periods analogous to the equity momentum experience, constructed on a 30 large-cap equal-weighted universe; establishes context and a further reason the momentum base effect needs a regime qualifier; establishes no dispersion-specific result. Grobys, Klaus and Sapkota, Niranjan (2019), "Cryptocurrencies and momentum", Economics Letters 180:6-10, abstract freely readable via the IDEAS/RePEc index, access 2; provides the contrary finding of no momentum in crypto over 2014-2018; establishes that the base effect is era-dependent; establishes no dispersion-specific result. Fracassi, Cesare and Kogan, Shimon, "Pure Momentum in Cryptocurrency Markets" (2022), free PDF, access 1; provides the behavioural mechanism for the momentum base effect — investors respond positively to changes in *reported* returns unrelated to any information release, so salience-driven buying generates pure momentum, with a long-short implementation on the two lowest and two highest 24-hour lagged returns reported at an annualised 538% and a Sharpe of 3.52 before costs; establishes a documented underreaction-to-salience mechanism and, by extension, a reason to expect momentum reliability to depend on how salient returns are; the reported pre-cost figures are not relied on as evidence of an edge. CoinGecko public API documentation, https://docs.coingecko.com/reference/coins-markets, checked 2026-10-20: access 2 for universe construction (the keyless public tier exposes current market caps and ranks with rate limits and a documented cache, not point-in-time history) and access 1 for current market-cap, volume and supply fields. Binance public data archive https://data.binance.vision, checked 2026-10-20: access 1 for spot klines including symbols subsequently delisted. Labels: published findings (the dispersion state-dependence, the power-law variance constraint, the momentum era-dependence, the salience mechanism) are separated from unverified assumptions (the dispersion definition, the 30-day formation window, the decile thresholds, the claim that a free-data point-in-time universe can be reconstructed, and the incremental value over Bitcoin realized volatility in a free replication).
