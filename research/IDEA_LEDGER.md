## CRYPTO-EDGE-001 — Basis/Carry Reversal (Cash-and-Carry Crash Predictor), v1.6

2. One-sentence thesis: Sustained elevated perpetual-futures basis (carry) relative to spot signals crowded leveraged longs and predicts elevated *crash conditionality* - a higher frequency and conditional severity of forward drawdowns, and a rise in sell liquidations as a share of open interest - over the following 1-4 weeks, because constrained arbitrage cannot immediately absorb the positioning imbalance.

The recorded total was corrected from 63 to the arithmetically correct 67 for the v1.2 dimension scores in the seventh cycle, re-scored to 61 in the same cycle, and is now 58. 4. Proposed edge family and target market/universe: Derivatives pricing/positioning anomaly (cost-of-carry / constrained arbitrage); BTC and ETH USDT-margined perpetuals versus spot on major retail-accessible venues (Binance, Bybit, OKX); cross-venue where public data permits.
5. Mechanism: When spot demand outpaces futures-arbitrage capacity, perp basis widens positive. The published version of this account (Schmeling, Schrimpf & Todorov) attributes crypto carry to two forces: (i) demand from smaller, trend-chasing investors seeking leveraged upside exposure and (ii) limited deployment of arbitrage capital because of regulatory and margin frictions, notably the absence of cross-margining and venue leverage caps (Binance cut maximum leverage to 20x in July 2021). Cash-and-carry arbitrage is constrained by absent cross-margining, funding and custody costs, and short availability, so the positioning imbalance persists; carry traders are themselves exposed to liquidation if prices move against the trade before convergence. Chi (2023) documents the basis as the strongest cross-sectional crypto futures factor, consistent with spot-to-futures information segmentation. Counter-mechanism now recorded explicitly: He, Manela, Ross & von Wachter (2026) report that past returns explain the majority of the futures-spot gap in a time-series regression, which indicates that the basis level is at least partly a momentum / positive-feedback proxy rather than a pure positioning measure; carry and momentum are not separable in the raw signal. Structural amendment (v1.2): the *measurement* of this mechanism differs by instrument in a way that materially changes the trigger population. Ruan and Streltsov (CFTC comment letter, 21 May 2025) state that perpetual derivatives "trade with a much smaller basis to the spot market due to the funding rate mechanism, allowing for closer tracking of spot prices", and Ackerer, Hugonnier and Jermann (NBER w32936) report that 75% of Bitcoin futures daily volume and 94% of open interest in the first half of 2023 came from perpetual futures. The published carry-crash evidence is estimated on dated futures (OKEx, CME), whose basis is not mechanically suppressed by a funding rule; on retail perpetuals the same economic state produces a much smaller observable basis. A second amendment (v1.2): the informational content of perp funding appears to be about *volatility* rather than *direction*. A 2026 Journal of Financial Markets paper ("Perpetual futures as predictors of Bitcoin volatility", PII S0927539826000939) finds funding-rate-based predictors materially improve forecasts of Bitcoin spot volatility, with the predictive contribution remaining significant after controlling for fixed-maturity futures volatility measures, and interprets funding as capturing "imbalanced leveraged positions whose subsequent unwinding and the associated price fluctuations unfold over extended periods". This supports the positioning mechanism but relocates its predicted payoff from the sign of the forward return to the dispersion of the forward return. **Amendment (v1.3): the crash mechanism in the perpetual era runs through basis *compression*, and the carry signal's sign is instrument-dependent.** The v1.2 mechanism attributes a crash to a *high* carry state, on the strength of dated-futures evidence. The October 2025 perpetual cascade is documented to have run the other way: the 30-day basis compressed from 6.9% to 4.5% while the cascade was in progress, the unwind of the hedged cash-and-carry book mechanically added selling pressure, and the futures led the crash with the basis swinging $1,367 from -$534 to +$833 in eight minutes while the mark price used for liquidation triggers undershot both spot and futures. Read together, these say two things. First, the mechanical amplification channel in the perpetual era is the *unwind* of the carry trade, which is a state of *falling* basis, not of elevated basis. Second, the basis is violently unstable at the crash instant, which is a warning against a level-based precursor: a variable that can swing $1,367 in eight minutes is not a calm state variable. A second amendment (v1.3) is about the sign itself: the *same research programme* produced an earlier working paper in which carry, measured as the funding rate of BTC/USD perpetual swaps rather than as dated-futures basis, **positively** predicts returns in the BTC time series and in a cross-section of 51 cryptocurrencies. The crash sign and the positive-return sign therefore belong to different instruments, and this entry's target instrument is the perpetual.
6. Exact falsifiable prediction: **Restructured in v1.2 into a tail claim and a withdrawn mean claim, because a direct preregistered test of the v1.1 mean-return version failed with the opposite sign.** (P1, retained, primary) When annualized BTC/ETH perp basis exceeds a preregistered positive threshold (theory-derived starting value +20%) for a sustained period, the *frequency and severity of crashes* over the next 1-4 weeks are elevated — defined as the probability and the conditional magnitude of a forward drawdown exceeding a preregistered threshold — and sell liquidations as % of open interest rise; when basis is negative or near zero, no prediction is made. Direction: basis high -> higher left-tail risk. Horizon: 1-4 weeks. (P2, withdrawn as a directional prediction) The v1.1 statement that "spot returns over the next 1-4 weeks are negative on average" is withdrawn as a preregistered directional claim: a preregistered test on Binance USDT-margined perpetuals over December 2019 to July 2026 found that high-basis days were followed by *higher* forward returns (+4.20% versus +1.59% at 7 days; +14.8% versus +6.1% at 30 days), a difference whose 95% block-bootstrap interval excluded zero on the wrong side. It is retained only as a quantity to be re-estimated, not as a trade direction. (P3, new requirement) Because the mechanism is now modelled as a volatility/instability channel, the prediction must be evaluated as a *tail* statistic (crash probability and conditional crash severity, or the variance of forward returns), and both the tail statistic and the mean-return difference must be reported; success on the tail statistic with a wrong-signed mean difference is a pass on P1, and failure on P1 cannot be excused by a mean-return result in either direction. (P4) The v1.1 addendum stands: the same prediction must survive when the signal is the momentum-residualized basis (basis minus the part of the basis explained by trailing returns), otherwise the effect is not attributable to positioning. **Amendment (v1.3): four requirements are added, and one trigger specification is withdrawn.** (P5, instrument specificity) The prediction is registered on *perpetual* basis; the published carry-crash evidence is registered on *dated futures* basis; and the two are not interchangeable, because the same authors' earlier perpetual-funding work reports the opposite mean sign. A validator must treat 'elevated perpetual basis' and 'elevated dated-futures basis' as two different states, must not import a published coefficient from one to the other, and must report the two instruments separately if both are tested. (P6, the published tail statistic) The peer-reviewed source this entry rests on does not test crash conditionality with a drawdown statistic at all. It tests it with *realized and implied skewness* and with *sell liquidations as a share of open interest*: a rise in carry of 10% predicts a 44% increase in total sell liquidations relative to total open interest over the next month, carry predicts sell but not long liquidations, and carry is strongly negatively related to implied skewness over the next one to three months. Those are the published tail statistics, and a validator that wants the published claim rather than a proxy must register one of them, with the option-implied variant (25-delta risk reversal) flagged as access 2 on free data. (P7, the carry-unwind arm) Because the documented 2025 amplification ran through basis compression and carry-trade unwinding, a separate arm must be registered for the *compression* state - basis falling by a preregistered size from a preregistered trailing high - as a crash-conditionality predictor, and the elevated-basis arm and the compression arm must be reported separately, because a crash in which the basis collapses is mechanically a different event from a crash in which the basis stays elevated. (P8, threshold) The +20% annualized threshold carried in v1.1 is documented to be unattainable on retail perpetuals in the current regime and is withdrawn as a trigger specification: the 30-day basis is reported at about 5.2% annualized against a 4.5% T-bill rate, the three-month basis has paid less than the two-year Treasury for 157 consecutive days since February 2026, and the perpetual basis is mechanically bounded by the funding-rate clamp, so the state the published work studied - carry up to 60% p.a. - is a dated-futures state that perps cannot reach. Thresholds are now to be preregistered by the validation owner in percentile terms, with trigger-state frequency reported separately for the pre- and post-July-2024 windows. This withdrawal is recorded as a parameter correction, not as a re-specification of the prediction: the entry's registered prediction has not itself been tested, so the falsification-handling rule - which forbids re-specification to dodge a failed test of a registered prediction - is not engaged.
7. Candidate explanatory variables and proposed signal concept: Signal = annualized perp basis (mark price minus spot index) / spot index, annualized using the funding/settlement cadence, computed at each 8-hour funding timestamp from only data known at that close (no look-ahead). Plain language: "is futures expensive relative to spot right now?" Forward window starts after the funding close at which the signal is observed.
8. Expected trade expression: Short perp (or reduce long exposure) when basis is extreme; optionally cash-and-carry unwind (long spot, short perp) where borrow and funding allow. Shorting is treated as a separate feasibility question (borrow cost, exchange rules). Not market-neutral by default; applied only in the basis-extreme regime.
9. Primary comparator or null explanation: Random-entry short with the same holding horizon and fee model; spot buy-and-hold over the same windows; basis computed on a venue-shuffled or lagged series as a placebo. High basis could also simply reflect rational cost of carry in a trending bull market, not crowded positioning. Mandatory added comparator (v1.1): a momentum-matched baseline (short when trailing returns are similarly elevated over the same window) and a momentum-residualized signal; if the momentum-matched baseline explains the effect, the positioning interpretation is not supported. Mandatory added comparator (v1.2): a *volatility-matched* baseline. Because perp funding has documented predictive content for forward spot volatility (Journal of Financial Markets, 2026), a validator must compare the high-basis state against states matched on realized volatility, not only on momentum. If high basis merely identifies high-volatility periods and the crash-risk conditionality vanishes once volatility is matched, then CRYPTO-EDGE-001 is a volatility-timing rule wearing a positioning label and the positioning interpretation is not supported. Two further mandatory controls (v1.2): a *carry-availability* control, because the cash-and-carry premium is documented to be effectively unpaid in the 2025-2026 regime (H62/H63 in the martex-quant ledger), so any test must report the trigger-state frequency separately in the pre- and post-2024 subsamples; and a *regime-split* control that reports the same statistics for the pre-July-2024 (published-sample) window and the post-July-2024 window, since the latter is the untested regime. Additional mandatory comparators (v1.3): (vii) an *open-interest-change* baseline, because a peer-reviewed panel study of Binance USDT-margined perpetuals for 2023-2024 finds that Bitcoin open-interest changes are the strongest crash predictor in a logit early-warning model (odds ratio 1.48, out-of-sample AUROC 0.677) - if the open-interest change explains the crash conditionality that the basis state explains, the basis adds nothing; (viii) a *carry-unwind* baseline for the P7 arm, matching on the size of the basis decline rather than on the basis level, so that the compression arm is not just the elevated arm with the sign flipped; and (ix) a *liquidation-direction* control, because the published source finds that carry predicts sell liquidations but not long liquidations, so the predicted liquidation direction must be reported rather than aggregated into a single liquidation figure.

13. Strongest argument against; likely failure regimes; confounders: **Ninth-cycle addition, now the second-strongest objection:** the crash mechanism this entry monetises is documented, in its two largest recent instances, to be *shock-driven and not anticipated from any pre-crash state*, which is close to the opposite of the entry's premise. Garcia Seuma (5 August 2026, arXiv:2608.03616) measures the branching ratio of the October 2025 record cascade in flight and finds it deeply subcritical (lambda-hat approximately 0.1), the transition abrupt and first-order rather than critical, with the in-cascade signature in the liquidity sector - price-impact spikes of 1.2-3.5x regressed and 3.2-9.1x quoted across two venues and two instruments, while open interest clears by 25-70% (-24.6% for Binance BTC, to 0.30-0.55 of baseline on Hyperliquid) - and shows that the branching model as an account of the *pre-cascade state* fails both of its falsifiable predictions with simulated power of at least 0.9. The companion paper (arXiv:2607.27070) adds that the price signature is present in five of seven cascades but silent in exactly the two sudden-news shocks, and that the October 2025 pattern does not replicate the August 2024 pattern but inverts it. A separate independent 2026 study finds that conditioning on the perpetual basis, funding rate, funding clock, volatility and session is flat in every case across 183 Binance pairs (Kitron and Wengrowicz 2026, arXiv:2608.21888), i.e. the state variable this entry uses carries no standalone information where it has been tested on a wide panel. None of these is a test of this entry's registered crash-conditionality claim, so the falsification-handling rule is not engaged and the entry is not rejected; but the prior that a positioning state can anticipate a crypto crash is now materially weaker than it was when the entry was written. (i) Basis can remain elevated through long trending bull markets. **Ninth-cycle adversarial duplicate check against the rejected CRYPTO-EDGE-002 (carried over from the eighth cycle's instruction and now performed):** the two are *adjacent but not duplicates*. 5. Mechanism: Perpetual funding rates are strongly but not perfectly correlated across venues because arbitrage across venues is segmented: exchanges do not offer cross-margining, so an arbitrageur exploiting a funding differential must fund and margin both legs separately, tying up capital on each venue and limiting the capital that can respond to any given discrepancy; venues also differ substantially in liquidity, and a venue-specific shock (withdrawal of market makers, collateral or stablecoin stress, venue-specific news) can produce a local imbalance. The literature documents that this residual dispersion persists: He, Manela, Ross & von Wachter (2026) report Binance-Bybit funding correlation of 0.74 with meaningful divergences during unusually large funding payments, and state explicitly that the residual cross-exchange dispersion reflects the limits of arbitrage across segmented venues and is a promising avenue for future research; Zhivkov (2026) finds that transaction costs and spread-reversal risks prevent arbitrage from eliminating large mispricings between platforms and that market frictions show up in cross-platform spreads rather than average levels, with persistent two-tier structure in which centralized venues lead price discovery. Two distinct stories can generate a high funding rate at one venue - locally crowded longs that will be force-unwound, or a locally scarce instrument whose market makers have withdrawn - and both imply the expensive venue's perpetual is biased rich relative to the integrated price; a third, contrary story is that the high-funding venue is the price-leading, information-rich venue, in which case the expensive venue should outperform. The hypothesis asserts the first two and is falsified by the third. **Amendment (v1.1): the third story is now the better-supported one, not the minority one.** Three independent sources place the price-discovery role on the more heavily traded, typically higher-funding venue rather than the thin one: Gornall, Rinaldi and Xiao (2025) report that perpetuals lead spot price discovery; Zhivkov (2026) finds that centralized venues lead price discovery with 61 percent higher information integration than decentralized venues and that information flow runs CEX to DEX with no reverse causality; and He, Manela, Ross and von Wachter (2026) attribute the residual cross-exchange funding dispersion to segmented arbitrage and liquidity differences. The mechanism that would make the *expensive* venue underperform therefore requires the thin venue to be the one with locally crowded longs, while the mechanism that makes the expensive venue *outperform* requires only that the expensive venue is the price leader — which is what the published evidence describes. A third, newly documented possibility (v1.1): the dispersion may be a *liquidity-withdrawal artifact* rather than a positioning imbalance at all, and the closest adjacent preregistered test of cross-venue disagreement as a magnitude found no predictive content whatsoever (see item 15). **Amendment (v1.2): the object the signal thresholds is documented to be fast-moving, and the price-discovery objection is documented to be weaker and less simple than v1.1 recorded.** Two independent external sources measure the half-life of a cross-venue funding differential directly, on free public data. A 2026 cross-venue study covering July 2024 to July 2026 across Binance, Bybit, OKX and Hyperliquid finds the exchange-versus-DEX funding spread mean-reverting with an AR(1) half-life of 1.5 to 3.5 hours, while the *direction* of the spread - which venue pays more - persists for months, and concludes explicitly that the signal's direction is a regime that lasts months while its level is hour-to-hour noise. The 2026 microstructure study by Zhivkov, Todorov and Georgiev, on 9.1 million hourly observations across 26 exchanges and 812 symbols from November 2025 to January 2026, finds intraday funding-spread autocorrelations above 0.96 with ETH reverting in about 20 minutes and other symbols taking hours to days, and finds that spreads peak roughly two hours after each eight-hour settlement and decay afterwards, with arbitrage prevalence of 21.4% at the 02:00 UTC peak against 16.5% at the 17:00 UTC trough. Two consequences follow, and they point in opposite directions. (a) Against the entry: the registered signal thresholds the *level* of the dispersion against a trailing distribution, and a quantity with a one-to-three-hour half-life means a trailing-decile extreme is close to trading noise, so the construction may be selecting on the wrong component of the phenomenon. (b) In favour of the entry: the price-discovery objection that has been the entry's primary opposing argument - 'the expensive venue is the price leader, so it outperforms' - is not a size story. The same 2026 study finds Bybit Granger-causing Binance funding rates in 62% of rolling windows against 21% in the reverse direction, and OKX Granger-causing Binance in 55% against 25%, and concludes that information discovery does not follow a size hierarchy and that mid-tier centralised venues and even decentralised venues contain information that propagates to larger platforms. A venue can therefore be expensive *and* informative for reasons unrelated to being the largest, and the entry's 'thin venue with locally crowded longs' story is not excluded by the objection. The v1.1 statement that the third story is 'now the better-supported one' is therefore withdrawn as a general claim: the evidence supports price leadership in the CEX-versus-DEX tier, and mid-tier leadership over the largest venue, and supports neither of the specific readings the entry's sign question needs.

(i) The signal may be a liquidity-supply artifact rather than a positioning imbalance - if market makers withdraw from a venue, funding there rises, and the premium may persist or widen rather than revert, making the trade a persistent cost drag. (ii) The sign may be the opposite of the stated prediction if the high-funding venue is the price-discovery leader (Gornall, Rinaldi & Xiao 2025 report perpetuals lead spot price discovery; Ackerer, Hugonnier & Jermann document perpetual pricing and price discovery effects), in which case buying the expensive venue and shorting the cheap one is the profitable expression. **Promoted to the primary objection in v1.1:** this is no longer the minority reading. It is corroborated by Zhivkov (2026) on CEX-to-DEX information flow with no reverse causality, by He et al. (2026) on liquidity-driven dispersion, and by a preregistered cross-venue test in which the high-premium venue was followed by *higher* returns in the continuation direction (+3.17% at 7 days, CI [+0.51%, +6.08%], breadth 17/20, on 31,752 symbol-days, surviving a trailing-return trim and a concentration check). If the sign is inverted, the trade in this entry loses money by construction and the entry is falsified in its registered form. (iii) Structural, permanent venue premia (fee tiers, funding caps, collateral composition, jurisdictional risk) may dominate the transient component and make cross-venue mean reversion a losing trade. (iv) Crowded-liquidity episodes can produce dispersion that resolves through a liquidation cascade at the expensive venue, where the relative move is large but the execution risk and slippage are worst - the classic adverse-selection problem. (v) Statistical power: extreme dispersion days are rare, so the effective sample is small and multiple-testing risk is high across venue pairs, symbols and thresholds. (vi) Data quality: mark-price staleness on thin venues, funding-interval mismatches, and stablecoin depegs can manufacture dispersion. (vii, new in v1.1) The closest adjacent empirical test of this entry's family found that cross-venue disagreement *as a magnitude* carries no forward-return content at all: in a preregistered test of three cross-venue price signals (raw premium, peg-adjusted premium, and the standard deviation of the three venues' log-USD prices), the dispersion signal was noise at all three horizons (1-day +0.08%, CI [-0.33%, +0.56%]; 7-day -0.24%, CI [-2.34%, +1.90%]; 30-day -6.65%, CI [-16.81%, +2.24%]). That test was on *price* dispersion at daily close rather than on *funding* dispersion, so it does not directly falsify this entry, but it removes dispersion magnitude as a self-evident predictor and means the funding-dispersion version now has to earn its own evidence. (viii, new in v1.1) Even a statistically significant information signal in this family may not be a tradable edge: the continuation signal from (ii) was carried into a strategy-grade test with next-open fills, fees, spread and participation impact, and was killed — Sharpe 0.89 against 1.0 required, deflated Sharpe 0.36 against 0.95 required, and it lost to an equal-weight buy-and-hold of the same basket on the same window and engine (Sharpe 0.94). That failure was for a long-only beta-heavy expression rather than for a delta-neutral relative trade, so it is an analogy and not a direct refutation, but it is a documented precedent for this exact family. Likely failure regimes: venue-specific distress, stablecoin depeg, regime changes in leverage caps, and periods when arbitrage capital is unconstrained (tight dispersion). (ix, new in v1.2) The signal may be measuring the wrong component of the phenomenon. The dispersion *level* has a documented one-to-three-hour half-life in a two-year, four-venue sample and the dispersion *direction* is a slow regime; a trailing-decile threshold on the level therefore selects on fast noise, and the entry's registered construction may fail for a reason that has nothing to do with its mechanism - it may simply have no power, in which case the test is not falsifying the mechanism but the construction, and a null result would be uninformative. (x, new in v1.2) The sharpest external test of the *family's* construction was not the sign test but a cost test, and it is directly adverse to this entry's registered construction: a two-year study of cross-venue funding carry across three centralised venues and one decentralised venue finds that a threshold rule which enters when the trailing spread clears a preregistered level earns the largest *gross* income of any rule tested and finishes at -9% to -15% annualized after costs, with execution consuming roughly three times the gross, while the rule that barely trades - hold continuously and revisit only which venue to short on a 30-day clock - returns +2.7% to +5.4% annualized net on notional with a maximum drawdown under 2%. Those are the entry's two candidate constructions and the documented outcome of the entry's own construction is a loss after costs. (xi, new in v1.2) Cross-asset heterogeneity of funding-dispersion effects is now documented and cuts both ways: a six-asset, three-venue study of funding extremes found the top-decile state predicting a negative median 72-hour return on BTC but positive medians on ETH, BNB, XRP and DOGE, so a claim stated over a multi-asset universe inherits a sign that depends on the asset mix; and a conditional study of 274 funding-extreme events found the response flipping with the direction of the market on the event day (reversal on up days, continuation on down days), which is a warning that a pooled estimate averages over two different regimes. (xii, new in v1.2) The strongest *supportive* finding for the entry is also new: the price-discovery objection, which v1.1 promoted to primary, is now documented to be a poor general rule, because mid-tier centralised venues and decentralised venues lead the largest venue in funding-rate information flow. The entry's contrarian story is therefore not excluded by the objection, but it remains entirely unsupported in its own right.

5. Mechanism: cross-sectional dispersion measures disagreement about the direction of the common factor, and it acts on the *reliability* of a ranking signal through two channels. (a) *Signal-to-noise channel*: the momentum payoff is delivered largely through the common (market) trend component; when dispersion is low, tokens co-move, the cross-sectional ranking is driven by differences in exposure and beta, and the ranking is stable and informative; when dispersion is high, the cross-section is dominated by idiosyncratic, asset-specific news (token unlocks, listings, meme rotations, idiosyncratic liquidations, protocol events), which raises the variance of the ranking error faster than it raises expected continuation, so the momentum spread's signal-to-noise ratio falls — and because crypto factor payoffs have power-law tails, a fall in signal-to-noise can push the mean below estimability rather than merely shrink it. (b) *Crowding channel*: high-dispersion states are also the states in which momentum positions are most crowded and most exposed to a common-factor reversal, so the momentum spread's *left tail* is fattest exactly in the high-dispersion regime. Why it may persist: the state variable is slow-moving relative to the formation window and is not itself the traded signal, so it does not attract the arbitrage that would erode the base momentum effect; it is a risk-budgeting switch on a known effect rather than an alpha source in its own right. Competing explanation that must be tested rather than assumed: dispersion may be a proxy for a *market-wide volatility regime* that independently damages momentum; the published evidence claims the effect is not explained by generic market volatility or by average cross-asset correlation, but a free replication must redo that orthogonalisation. **Amendment (v1.1): the state variable and the signal are unchanged; what changed is the universe in which the claim can be measured, and the answer to the entry's own open question about that universe is now known.** The v1.0 mechanism argued that cross-sectional dispersion raises the variance of the ranking error faster than it raises expected continuation, so that the momentum spread's signal-to-noise ratio falls. Two new findings bear on it. First, the measurement of the *base* effect: a preregistered audit of an unchanged momentum-rotation specification, re-run on a universe selected point-in-time rather than at the end of the sample, retained 58% of its Sharpe (1.47 to 0.86) and 49% of its CAGR (+42.91% to +21.06%), cleared neither a Sharpe of 1.0 nor a deflated-Sharpe bar of 0.95, and reversed its best year (2026: +92.80% to -18.61%). The momentum book that this entry proposes to switch is therefore materially weaker on an honest universe, and the degradation concentrated in exactly the years when a coin that later became famous was mid-parabola - that is, in the parabolic, high-dispersion years. Second, the *conditional* claim: a preregistered test in the same ledger conditioned the cross-sectional momentum spread on the cross-sectional dispersion of trailing 30-day returns - the same state variable, the same book, a different universe, terciles instead of deciles and a 7-day forward window instead of a 1-month one - and it was killed, with the spread monotone *increasing* across dispersion terciles (+0.50%, +1.05%, +1.40%) and a top-minus-bottom difference of +0.90% with a 95% block-bootstrap interval of [-2.66%, +3.69%]. The observed direction is the opposite of this entry's registered prediction, which says the spread should be *smaller* in the high-dispersion regime. The test is not decisive - the interval is wide, the universe was a hindsight one, and the state variable was measured daily rather than on a trailing window - but it is the closest available test and it does not support the entry.

13. Strongest argument against; likely failure regimes; confounders: (i) **the base effect may not exist in a tradeable form** — Grobys and Sapkota (2019) find no momentum in crypto for 2014-2018, and Grobys and Shahzad (2026) show that the realised variances of six crypto momentum strategies follow power laws so that the population mean and variance are statistically not defined under block bootstrap, concluding that variance-based performance metrics are uninformative; a conditional overlay on a base effect whose mean may be unestimable is a weak proposition. (ii) **the incremental content may be nil** — the published claim is that dispersion adds information beyond Bitcoin realized volatility and average cross-asset correlation; if the crypto momentum spread is already dominated by generic market state, dispersion is a noisy proxy for it. (iii) **universe construction drives the answer** — the published work states that inference is sensitive to dynamic universe construction, and a free-data implementation is more exposed to survivorship than the original. (iv) **heavy tails cut both ways** — in the high-dispersion regime the momentum spread's left tail is fattest, so the mean can fall even where the median does not, which makes a mean-based test the wrong instrument and can make a real effect look like a null. (v) **confounding with launches and unlocks** — a high-dispersion state is often produced by a small number of new or newly issued tokens entering the universe, so the state variable may be a proxy for "the universe changed" rather than for disagreement. (vi) **crowding** — the momentum book is the most crowded cross-sectional book in crypto, so the high-dispersion regime is also when it is most likely to be unwound together, which is an argument for the overlay and a confound for its attribution. Likely failure regimes: low-volatility, high-momentum periods where the overlay would suppress the base effect; a universe whose membership churns; any window where the top-decile winners are driven by a single idiosyncratic token. (vii, new in v1.1) The base effect is materially smaller than the published state's own framing implies. A preregistered audit of an unchanged momentum-rotation specification found that roughly 40% of its measured Sharpe and half of its CAGR came from being allowed to rank coins that were in the universe only because of what happened later, with the degradation concentrated in the incumbent's biggest years and one year reversing sign; the honest Sharpe was at most 0.86 and 'plausibly lower', and the residual was stated to be an upper bound because delisted coins cannot enter. The entry's own published source had separately warned that inference about cryptocurrency momentum is sensitive to dynamic universe construction, which is precisely the variable that determines the answer. (viii, new in v1.1) The entry's incremental claim is now contradicted in direction by the closest available test: the dispersion dial was killed with a point estimate leaning the opposite way, and the breadth-dial sibling was killed on the same pattern; the family's two preregistered tests are both null or adverse. (ix, new in v1.1) The free-data standard cannot be met for the essential input in the tested regime, and the reason is structural rather than a matter of finding the right source: the delisting half of survivorship is not observable on free data, and the market-cap screen that would be the natural substitute is not available in history either.
