# IDEA LEDGER

This is the complete catalogue of research-only systematic cryptocurrency edge hypotheses. No empirical validation is performed in this repository.

Stable IDs: CRYPTO-EDGE-001, CRYPTO-EDGE-002, CRYPTO-EDGE-003.

Scoring convention used in this file: for each rubric dimension, contribution = (score / 5) x dimension weight (weights in percentage points), so the seven contributions sum to a total out of 100. Cycle 1 records applied this formula incorrectly to the two 10%-weight dimensions (falsifiability and originality were each entered at half their proper value); all totals below use the stated formula. ## CRYPTO-EDGE-001 — Basis/Carry Reversal (Cash-and-Carry Crash Predictor), v1.1

1. Short name and version: Basis/Carry Reversal v1.1.
2. One-sentence thesis: Sustained elevated perpetual-futures basis (carry) relative to spot signals crowded leveraged longs and predicts elevated sell-liquidation risk and negative spot returns over the following weeks, because constrained arbitrage cannot immediately absorb the positioning imbalance.
3. Status: shortlisted.
4. Proposed edge family and target market/universe: Derivatives pricing/positioning anomaly (cost-of-carry / constrained arbitrage); BTC and ETH USDT-margined perpetuals versus spot on major retail-accessible venues (Binance, Bybit, OKX); cross-venue where public data permits.
5. Mechanism: When spot demand outpaces futures-arbitrage capacity, perp basis widens positive. The published version of this account (Schmeling, Schrimpf & Todorov) attributes crypto carry to two forces: (i) demand from smaller, trend-chasing investors seeking leveraged upside exposure and (ii) limited deployment of arbitrage capital because of regulatory and margin frictions, notably the absence of cross-margining and venue leverage caps (Binance cut maximum leverage to 20x in July 2021). Cash-and-carry arbitrage is constrained by absent cross-margining, funding and custody costs, and short availability, so the positioning imbalance persists; carry traders are themselves exposed to liquidation if prices move against the trade before convergence. Chi (2023) documents the basis as the strongest cross-sectional crypto futures factor, consistent with spot-to-futures information segmentation. Counter-mechanism now recorded explicitly: He, Manela, Ross & von Wachter (2026) report that past returns explain the majority of the futures-spot gap in a time-series regression, which indicates that the basis level is at least partly a momentum / positive-feedback proxy rather than a pure positioning measure; carry and momentum are not separable in the raw signal.
6. Exact falsifiable prediction: When annualized BTC/ETH perp basis exceeds a preregistered positive threshold (theory-derived starting value +20%) for a sustained period, spot returns over the next 1-4 weeks are negative on average and sell liquidations as % of open interest rise; when basis is negative or near zero, no directional prediction is made. Direction: basis high -> down. Horizon: 1-4 weeks. Addendum (v1.1): the same prediction must also survive when the signal is the momentum-residualized basis (basis minus the part of the basis explained by trailing returns), otherwise the effect is not attributable to positioning.
7. Candidate explanatory variables and proposed signal concept: Signal = annualized perp basis (mark price minus spot index) / spot index, annualized using the funding/settlement cadence, computed at each 8-hour funding timestamp from only data known at that close (no look-ahead). Plain language: "is futures expensive relative to spot right now?" Forward window starts after the funding close at which the signal is observed.
8. Expected trade expression: Short perp (or reduce long exposure) when basis is extreme; optionally cash-and-carry unwind (long spot, short perp) where borrow and funding allow. Shorting is treated as a separate feasibility question (borrow cost, exchange rules). Not market-neutral by default; applied only in the basis-extreme regime.
9. Primary comparator or null explanation: Random-entry short with the same holding horizon and fee model; spot buy-and-hold over the same windows; basis computed on a venue-shuffled or lagged series as a placebo. High basis could also simply reflect rational cost of carry in a trending bull market, not crowded positioning. Mandatory added comparator (v1.1): a momentum-matched baseline (short when trailing returns are similarly elevated over the same window) and a momentum-residualized signal; if the momentum-matched baseline explains the effect, the positioning interpretation is not supported.
10. Data required, source URLs, free-access classification, granularity, likely history, coverage, limitations: perpetual mark price, spot index price, open interest, long/short liquidation notionals, funding rates; 8-hourly or daily; history at least 2 years. Sources for replication with free public endpoints (verified 2026-10-20): Binance GET https://fapi.binance.com/fapi/v1/fundingRate (public, no key, limit max 1000, includes fundingTime and markPrice; funding interval/cap/floor changes via GET /fapi/v1/fundingInfo); Bybit GET https://api.bybit.com/v5/market/funding/history (public, no key, 200 rows/page, USDT/USDC/inverse perps); OKX GET https://www.okx.com/api/v5/public/funding-rate-history (public, capped at 100 rows/call); Binance public data archive (https://data.binance.vision, documented in https://github.com/binance/binance-public-data) for klines/trades. Access classification: 1 (confirmed free, public) for funding, mark price and spot on major venues; 2 (apparently free, unverified depth/rate limits) for multi-year REST pagination depth, open interest, and liquidation notional detail. Note: the published carry-crash evidence (BIS WP 1087 / Management Science) used commercial vendor data (Skew, Coinmetrics); that is a source of the published result, not a requirement for a free replication.
11. Plausible friction and capacity considerations (theoretical, no measured values): funding paid on the short side erodes carry; short availability and borrow costs; slippage entering during stress; exchange leverage caps and liquidation of the arbitrage trade itself before convergence; turnover is limited to basis-extreme regimes so capacity is modest. No friction values are invented.
12. Why meaningfully different from the catalogue and prior art: Grounded in documented basis premium (Chi 2023) and carry-crash predictability (BIS WP 1087 / Management Science 2026), with a precise falsifiable liquidation-linked prediction and an explicit momentum-residualization requirement. Distinct from plain moving-average crossovers, from CRYPTO-EDGE-002 (level of cost of carry vs funding-flow extremes), and from CRYPTO-EDGE-003 (absolute time-series carry on a benchmark venue vs cross-venue relative dispersion).
13. Strongest argument against; likely failure regimes; confounders: (i) Basis can remain elevated through long trending bull markets (carry may be profitable while the trend continues). (ii) The published crash-predictive relation is estimated on dated futures (OKEx, CME) with data from Skew; transfer to perps on retail venues is untested, and Gornall, Rinaldi & Xiao (2025) report that perpetuals now dominate crypto derivatives volume and reduce extreme price dislocations, which mechanically shrinks the population of extreme-basis trigger states in the perp era. (iii) Crashes may be driven by exogenous news rather than positioning; Garcia Seuma (2026) finds that pre-crash signatures are event-heterogeneous and that the most attractive in-sample pattern from the October 2025 record cascade inverts out of sample on the August 2024 cascade, and that a pure news shock (February 2025 tariff episode) shows no precursor in any variable - a direct warning for any single-variable crash precursor. (iv) Liquidation data is noisy and often proxied. (v) Momentum explains much of the basis, so the raw signal may be a momentum proxy. Likely failure regimes: strong trending markets, exogenous shock events, regime change in venue leverage rules.
14. Research-only validation plan to be executed elsewhere: preselect the basis threshold; define entry at funding close and exit at subsequent funding closes over 1-4 weeks; baseline versus random entry and versus momentum-matched entry; report both raw-basis and momentum-residualized signal variants; theoretical friction cost model (funding, spread, slippage, borrow); out-of-sample split with an explicit post-July-2024 subsample; regime coverage (bull, bear, sideways, liquidation-event windows); leave-one-venue-out check; falsification criteria: no negative forward return after high basis, no rise in sell liquidations as % of OI, or the effect vanishing once momentum is controlled. Do not execute this plan in this repository.
15. Evidence notes with sources and dates: BIS Working Paper 1087, "Crypto carry", free PDF https://www.bis.org/publ/work1087.pdf (April 2023, revised October 2025), checked 2026-10-20 - high carry predicts spot crashes and rising sell liquidations; published version: Schmeling, Schrimpf & Todorov (2026), "Crypto Carry", Management Science, doi 10.1287/mnsc.2024.05069, published online 6 May 2026 - abstract accessible free (https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/mnsc.2024.05069), full text access classification 2; the authors' own summary column (CEPR VoxEU, 22 Dec 2025) states the sample runs March 2019 to July 2024 for OKEx with data from Skew and Coinmetrics, and the published data description states daily data March 2019 to July 2024 (checked 2026-10-20). This supersedes the earlier characterisation in cycles 1-3 that the sample ended January 2022: that was true of the March 2023 working-paper version, and the "post-2022 validity gap" is now materially narrower (the published sample includes 2022-2024) while a new gap opens after July 2024. He, Manela, Ross & von Wachter, "Fundamentals of Perpetual Futures", arXiv:2212.06888 (v7, 17 Sep 2026), free - no-arbitrage benchmark for perps; deviations from it are larger than in FX, co-move across currencies and diminish over time; past returns explain more than half of the futures-spot gap in a time-series regression, indicating positive feedback/momentum behaviour (v5/v6 text). Gornall, Rinaldi & Xiao (2025), "Perpetual Futures and Basis Risk: Evidence from Cryptocurrency", SSRN 5036933 (rev. 19 May 2025) and AEA 2026 program - perps dominate volume, enhance liquidity, reduce extreme price dislocations, arbitrage drawdowns on perps mean-revert quickly. Garcia Seuma (2026), arXiv:2607.27070, free - seven BTC perpetual liquidation cascades 2022-2025; out-of-sample inversion; exogenous shocks show no precursor. Cao, Zhai & Luo (2024), "Anatomy of cryptocurrency perpetual futures returns", Journal of Futures Markets - 134 return predictors, basis and price-volume factors explain all 48 significant strategies (abstract free, full text access unverified). Labels: published findings and mechanistic reasoning are separated from unverified assumptions (the +20% threshold, the sustained-period rule, and the mapping of published dated-futures results onto retail perps are assumptions, not evidence).
16. Scores, total rank, confidence, explanation: Causal mechanism 4/5 -> 16; evidence quality 4/5 -> 12; robustness 3/5 -> 9; free-data accessibility 5/5 -> 15; net value after frictions 3/5 -> 9; falsifiability 4/5 -> 8; originality 2/5 -> 4. Total 73/100. The cycle-1 record stated 67; the 6-point difference is the corrected application of the rubric formula to the two 10%-weight dimensions, not new evidence. No dimension score was changed in v1.1: the extended published sample (to July 2024) improved the evidence base but the added contrary evidence (momentum explanation of the basis, perp-era dislocation reduction, event-heterogeneous crash precursors) holds robustness at 3/5 and net value at 3/5, so the ranking is unchanged. Confidence: medium - mechanism and published evidence are solid and now peer-reviewed, but originality is low (cash-and-carry is well documented), the signal is confounded with momentum, and transfer of the published dated-futures result to retail perpetual venues is untested. Rank 1: high as a baseline/comparator, moderate as a novel edge.
17. Next most valuable research question: does the carry-crash relation persist beyond the end of the published sample (after July 2024) and does it transfer from dated futures (OKEx/CME) to perpetuals on current retail venues - and does a momentum-residualized basis predict crashes better than the raw basis? Resolution would either confirm a durable post-2024 effect or show the relation was a pre-2022 regime artefact; this single question now dominates the research priority of the catalogue.

## CRYPTO-EDGE-002 — Funding Extreme Contrarian (Crowded Leverage Unwind), v1.1

1. Short name and version: Funding Extreme Contrarian v1.1.
2. One-sentence thesis: Extreme funding-rate readings (top/bottom decile of the trailing distribution) signal overcrowded leveraged positioning prone to forced unwind, predicting short-horizon mean reversion opposite to the funding extreme.
3. Status: shortlisted (rank 3).
4. Proposed edge family and target market/universe: Derivatives positioning/flow anomaly (crowded leverage, forced liquidation feedback); BTC and ETH perpetuals on retail-accessible venues with public funding history.
5. Mechanism: Positive funding means longs pay shorts - crowded longs. When sentiment turns, leveraged longs hit maintenance margin; the exchange emits forced, price-insensitive market sells, pushing price further down into the next thresholds - the leverage-manufactured feedback documented in the liquidation-cascade literature (Garcia Seuma 2026; BitMEX study showing ~60X average leverage among the liquidated and substantial daily liquidation %). Crowding persists because momentum chasers enter late and leverage is sticky. Mirror image for extreme negative funding (crowded shorts, short-squeeze exposure). Structural amendment (v1.1): the funding rate is by construction proportional to the futures-spot gap, so a gap driven by momentum / positive feedback generates the same funding extremes as a gap driven by crowding; the mechanism therefore does not by itself distinguish this hypothesis from a momentum-reversal rule, and the perp-era compression of dislocations means extreme funding states are rarer and less extreme than in the pre-2022 sample.
6. Exact falsifiable prediction: Funding rate in the top decile of the trailing 90-day distribution predicts negative returns over the next 1-7 days; bottom decile predicts positive returns. Direction: opposite to funding sign. Horizon: 1-7 days. Addendum (v1.1): the prediction must hold incrementally after controlling for CRYPTO-EDGE-001 basis and for trailing returns; if the decile signal adds nothing beyond those, it is not a distinct edge.
7. Candidate explanatory variables and proposed signal concept: Signal = 8-hour funding rate from public exchange data, expressed as a percentile of the trailing 90-day distribution computed using only past data (no look-ahead); signal observed at a funding timestamp, forward window starts after. Plain language: "are traders paying an extreme premium to hold one side?"
8. Expected trade expression: Contrarian - short when funding is extremely positive, long when extremely negative; shorting feasibility is a separate question (borrow, exchange constraints). Not market-neutral; avoid strong trending news regimes as a filter. Alignment note: when contrarian direction coincides with receiving funding, the carry helps rather than hurts.
9. Primary comparator or null explanation: Random entry with the same hold period; spot buy-and-hold; funding series time-shuffled as a placebo; CRYPTO-EDGE-001 (basis level) as an alternative positioning proxy to test whether funding adds incremental information; momentum-matched entry (v1.1, mandatory) given that the funding rate is proportional to the futures-spot gap.
10. Data required: 8-hour funding rates, mark price, spot price; open interest as context; history at least 2 years; free public endpoints verified 2026-10-20 - Binance /fapi/v1/fundingRate, Bybit /v5/market/funding/history, OKX /api/v5/public/funding-rate-history, Binance public data archive for klines. Access classification: 1 (confirmed free, public) for funding and price on major venues; 2 (granularity/history limits unverified) for open interest and liquidation detail.
11. Plausible friction and capacity considerations: funding receipt is favorable in the contrarian direction when sign-aligned, but adverse when it is not; slippage and whipsaws in strong trends can dominate; exchange-specific funding formula and clip; exchange leverage rules affect how quickly unwinds occur. No measured friction values.
12. Why meaningfully different from the catalogue and prior art: Mechanism is funding-rate flow extremes and forced-unwind feedback, distinct from the carry level in CRYPTO-EDGE-001 and from the cross-venue relative measure in CRYPTO-EDGE-003. Distinct from generic price oscillators (e.g. RSI overbought) because it uses derivatives positioning, not price history alone - but note (v1.1) that the informational content of the funding extreme overlaps materially with momentum.
13. Strongest argument against; likely failure regimes; confounders: (i) Extremes can persist for extended trending periods (funding can stay positive for months in a bull run). (ii) Timing of the unwind is unpredictable; false positives are frequent. (iii) Little rigorous published evidence ties funding percentiles specifically to forward returns - most sources are practitioner/anecdotal. (iv) He et al. (2026) show the futures-spot gap is largely explained by past returns, so a funding extreme is largely a momentum extreme; the contrarian rule may be a momentum-reversal rule wearing a derivatives label. (v) Zhivkov (2026) reports funding rates clustering near zero across venues (interquartile range 1.20 bps for centralized venues), so genuine extremes are rare events and any percentile threshold will be estimated on a thin tail. (vi) Gornall, Rinaldi & Xiao (2025) report that perpetuals reduce extreme price dislocations, which mechanically shrinks the trigger population in the perp era. (vii) Garcia Seuma (2026) shows liquidation timing is not predictable from a stable precursor, and that a news-driven cascade shows no precursor at all. Likely failure regimes: strong momentum regimes, exogenous news shocks.
14. Research-only validation plan to be executed elsewhere: preselect decile thresholds; entry at funding close once an extreme is confirmed using only past data; hold 1-7 days; baselines versus random entry, momentum-matched entry, and CRYPTO-EDGE-001 basis-controlled entry; friction model includes funding receipt (sign-dependent) and slippage; out-of-sample test with a post-2022-only subsample; regime coverage; falsification: no mean reversion after extremes, extremes persisting through the trend, or no incremental power over basis and momentum. Do not execute here.
15. Evidence notes with sources and dates: Garcia Seuma (2026) arXiv:2607.27070 (v1, 30 Jul 2026) and arXiv:2608.03616, both free - forced-liquidation feedback and cascade mechanics; the newer reading adds that pre-crash signatures are event-heterogeneous, that the October 2025 in-sample pattern inverts on the August 2024 cascade, and that the February 2025 news shock shows no precursor (checked 2026-10-20). arXiv:2102.04591 BitMEX leverage/liquidation study, free - substantial forced liquidations, ~60X average leverage among liquidated; single-venue sample ending Feb 2021. He, Manela, Ross & von Wachter (2026) arXiv:2212.06888v7 - funding is proportional to the futures-spot gap; deviations diminish over time; momentum explains much of the gap (checked 2026-10-20). Zhivkov (2026), "The Two-Tiered Structure of Cryptocurrency Funding Rate Markets", Mathematics 14(2):346, doi 10.3390/math14020346, open access - funding clusters near zero (IQR 1.20 bps CEX); frictions manifest in cross-platform spreads rather than average levels (checked 2026-10-20). Gornall, Rinaldi & Xiao (2025) SSRN 5036933 - perps reduce extreme price dislocations. Gate.io Wiki funding/OI/liquidation article - free but exchange-published and marketing-adjacent - treated as anecdotal, not evidence. Labels: published findings vs unverified assumptions (decile thresholds, the trailing window, and the incremental-power claim are assumptions).
16. Scores, total rank, confidence, explanation: Causal mechanism 3/5 -> 12; evidence quality 2/5 -> 6; robustness 2/5 -> 6; free-data accessibility 5/5 -> 15; net value after frictions 3/5 -> 9; falsifiability 3/5 -> 6; originality 2/5 -> 4. Total 58/100. The cycle-1 record stated 53; the 5-point difference is the corrected rubric formula (with cycle-1 dimension scores the corrected total would have been 61). Robustness was lowered 3/5 -> 2/5 in v1.1 on new contrary evidence (perp-era dislocation compression, funding clustering near zero, momentum confounding) and confidence lowered from low-medium to low. Rank 3: below CRYPTO-EDGE-001 and below CRYPTO-EDGE-003, worth keeping only if it shows incremental predictability beyond basis and momentum.
17. Next most valuable research question: does funding-extreme contrarian performance survive control for CRYPTO-EDGE-001 basis and for trailing returns - and does the frequency of true funding extremes (tail mass) in 2024-2026 provide enough independent observations for a preregistered decile test to be meaningful at all?

## CRYPTO-EDGE-003 — Cross-Venue Funding Dispersion (Fragmentation Relative Value), v1.0

1. Short name and version: Cross-Venue Funding Dispersion v1.0.
2. One-sentence thesis: Extreme dispersion of perpetual funding rates across trading venues for the same asset identifies venue-specific positioning or liquidity imbalances that the integrated multi-venue market has not priced, so the venue with the anomalously high funding rate subsequently underperforms the cross-venue median perpetual on a 5-20 day horizon, with the funding differential persisting long enough to be partly harvestable.
3. Status: new (shortlisted, rank 2).
4. Proposed edge family and target market/universe: Cross-venue market-fragmentation / relative-value derivatives (segmented arbitrage, venue-specific liquidity and counterparty premia); BTC and ETH USDT-margined perpetuals on Binance, Bybit and OKX (extendable to other venues where funding history and volume filters are available).
5. Mechanism: Perpetual funding rates are strongly but not perfectly correlated across venues because arbitrage across venues is segmented: exchanges do not offer cross-margining, so an arbitrageur exploiting a funding differential must fund and margin both legs separately, tying up capital on each venue and limiting the capital that can respond to any given discrepancy; venues also differ substantially in liquidity, and a venue-specific shock (withdrawal of market makers, collateral or stablecoin stress, venue-specific news) can produce a local imbalance. The literature documents that this residual dispersion persists: He, Manela, Ross & von Wachter (2026) report Binance-Bybit funding correlation of 0.74 with meaningful divergences during unusually large funding payments, and state explicitly that the residual cross-exchange dispersion reflects the limits of arbitrage across segmented venues and is a promising avenue for future research; Zhivkov (2026) finds that transaction costs and spread-reversal risks prevent arbitrage from eliminating large mispricings between platforms and that market frictions show up in cross-platform spreads rather than average levels, with persistent two-tier structure in which centralized venues lead price discovery. Two distinct stories can generate a high funding rate at one venue - locally crowded longs that will be force-unwound, or a locally scarce instrument whose market makers have withdrawn - and both imply the expensive venue's perpetual is biased rich relative to the integrated price; a third, contrary story is that the high-funding venue is the price-leading, information-rich venue, in which case the expensive venue should outperform. The hypothesis asserts the first two and is falsified by the third.
6. Exact falsifiable prediction: Define d_v,t = (venue-v 8-hour funding rate for symbol s at funding timestamp t, normalized to a common 8-hour-equivalent basis) minus (the cross-venue median funding rate for the same symbol at the same timestamp). When d_v,t is in the top decile of its trailing 90-day distribution (and, in the mirrored case, the bottom decile), the venue-v perpetual's return over the next 5 to 20 days is below (above) the median return of the same symbol's perpetuals across the other venues, and the funding differential itself does not fully close within one to three funding intervals. Direction: high relative funding -> relative underperformance of the expensive venue. Horizon: 5-20 days for relative performance; 1-3 funding intervals for dispersion persistence.
7. Candidate explanatory variables and proposed signal concept: Signal = cross-venue funding dispersion d_v,t (a relative, venue-demeaned quantity), computed only from funding rates settled at or before timestamp t; venue pair selection and all thresholds from trailing distributions only; forward window starts after the funding settlement. Plain language: "is this one venue's perpetual unusually expensive relative to the same asset priced elsewhere?" Because the measure differences out the common component of carry - which He et al. show is largely momentum-driven and co-moving across currencies - it targets venue-specific segmentation rather than market-wide carry.
8. Expected trade expression: Market-neutral relative value: short the expensive venue's perpetual and long the cheap venue's perpetual (or hedge the short with spot on the same venue), equal notional, no directional beta; the funding received on the short leg offsets carry paid on the long leg when the differential is positive. Not a directional long/short market bet. Feasibility caveats: margin must be posted on both venues, collateral must exist on both, and venue or collateral risk (for example a stablecoin depeg or venue insolvency) breaks the hedge rather than being diversifiable.
9. Primary comparator or null explanation: (i) a random venue pair traded with the same notional and dates; (ii) the same trade restricted to low-dispersion dates; (iii) the liquidity-artifact null - the dispersion must survive filtering venues by volume and open interest and using mark prices rather than last trades; (iv) the momentum null - the signal must add value after residualizing against trailing returns and against CRYPTO-EDGE-001 basis; (v) the price-discovery null, in which the expensive venue leads and the trade should be reversed; (vi) a sign placebo using the mirrored dispersion signal to detect implementation errors.
10. Data required, source URLs, free-access classification, granularity, likely history, coverage, limitations: per-venue 8-hour funding history and per-venue perpetual mark prices and klines for the same symbol; per-venue volume/open interest for liquidity filters. Verified free public sources (checked 2026-10-20): Binance GET https://fapi.binance.com/fapi/v1/fundingRate (public, no key, limit max 1000, returns fundingRate, fundingTime, markPrice; funding interval/cap/floor adjustments via GET /fapi/v1/fundingInfo) - access 1 for endpoint availability, 2 for multi-year pagination depth; Bybit GET https://api.bybit.com/v5/market/funding/history (public, no key, 200 rows per page, covers USDT/USDC/inverse perps) - access 1; OKX GET https://www.okx.com/api/v5/public/funding-rate-history (public, capped at 100 rows per call) - access 1 for endpoint availability, 2 for retention depth; Binance public data archive https://data.binance.vision (documented at https://github.com/binance/binance-public-data) for monthly/daily klines and trades - access 1. Limitations: funding intervals, caps and floors differ across venues and must be normalized (Binance /fapi/v1/fundingInfo exists for this); collateral mixes (USDT vs USDC) and stablecoin depegs can masquerade as basis; venue survivorship (FTX, OKEx renames) must be handled; thin-venue quotes and stale mark prices create artificial dispersion; funding timestamps must be aligned across venues with different settlement cadences.
11. Plausible friction and capacity considerations (theoretical, no measured values): the trade needs margin on two venues, so capital efficiency is roughly halved versus a single-venue trade; taker fees on both legs and possible slippage in exactly the stressed conditions that trigger the signal; funding accrual is the main expected return but Zhivkov (2026) documents that among top cross-venue opportunities only about 40 percent remained profitable after transaction costs and spread reversals, with forced exits in about 95 percent of opportunities - so the pure carry-capture version of this trade is likely unprofitable at retail scale and the hypothesis is stated as a directional relative-performance claim instead; transfer and counterparty risk between venues is unhedgeable; capacity is limited by the depth of the smaller venue.
12. Why meaningfully different from the catalogue and prior art: CRYPTO-EDGE-001 is an absolute time-series carry level on a benchmark instrument aimed at crash timing; CRYPTO-EDGE-002 is a single-venue funding percentile contrarian rule; CRYPTO-EDGE-003 is a relative, cross-sectional, market-neutral measure of venue-specific fragmentation that differences out the market-wide carry and momentum component. Novelty is scoped to this catalogue and to the searches performed (see SOURCE_NOTES.md): the primary literature explicitly identifies residual cross-exchange funding dispersion as an open research avenue rather than a studied effect, and the directional relative-performance mapping from dispersion to venue-level underperformance has not been found in the free sources consulted. It is not claimed to be globally novel.
13. Strongest argument against; likely failure regimes; confounders: (i) The signal may be a liquidity-supply artifact rather than a positioning imbalance - if market makers withdraw from a venue, funding there rises, and the premium may persist or widen rather than revert, making the trade a persistent cost drag. (ii) The sign may be the opposite of the stated prediction if the high-funding venue is the price-discovery leader (Gornall, Rinaldi & Xiao 2025 report perpetuals lead spot price discovery; Ackerer, Hugonnier & Jermann document perpetual pricing and price discovery effects), in which case buying the expensive venue and shorting the cheap one is the profitable expression. (iii) Structural, permanent venue premia (fee tiers, funding caps, collateral composition, jurisdictional risk) may dominate the transient component and make cross-venue mean reversion a losing trade. (iv) Crowded-liquidity episodes can produce dispersion that resolves through a liquidation cascade at the expensive venue, where the relative move is large but the execution risk and slippage are worst - the classic adverse-selection problem. (v) Statistical power: extreme dispersion days are rare, so the effective sample is small and multiple-testing risk is high across venue pairs, symbols and thresholds. (vi) Data quality: mark-price staleness on thin venues, funding-interval mismatches, and stablecoin depegs can manufacture dispersion. Likely failure regimes: venue-specific distress, stablecoin depeg, regime changes in leverage caps, and periods when arbitrage capital is unconstrained (tight dispersion).
14. Research-only validation plan to be executed elsewhere: preregister the venue universe (with liquidity filters fixed in advance), the extreme-dispersion threshold (trailing 90-day decile of d_v,t), the holding window (5-20 days) and the persistence window (1-3 funding intervals); enter at the funding settlement after observation, sized delta-neutral across the two venues; comparators as in item 9, including the reversed-sign placebo; cost model must include taker fees on both legs, funding accrual on both legs, slippage, and the capital cost of posting margin on two venues; out-of-sample design with time-based splits and a leave-one-venue-out test (fit on some venues, trade the held-out venue); regime coverage across trending bull, bear, high-volatility and liquidation-event windows; falsification criteria - dispersion closes fully within one funding interval, or the expensive venue does not underperform on average, or the effect disappears after volume/OI filtering and mark-price substitution, or the effect vanishes after residualizing against momentum and basis, or the reversed-sign placebo performs as well as the stated signal. Do not execute this plan in this repository.

Evidence notes with sources and dates: He, Manela, Ross & Wachter (2026), "Fundamentals of Perpetual Futures", arXiv:2212.06888 (v7, 17 Sep 2026), confirmed free - funding rates tend to be similar across exchanges because of cross-exchange arbitrage but can diverge in extreme liquidity episodes; the residual dispersion is attributed to segmented arbitrage (no cross-margining, each leg separately funded and margined) and to liquidity differences across exchanges; Binance-Bybit funding correlation of 0.74 with meaningful divergences during large funding payments; the authors state that residual cross-exchange dispersion reflects limits to arbitrage across segmented venues and is a promising avenue for future research (mechanism evidence and prior-art scoping).
