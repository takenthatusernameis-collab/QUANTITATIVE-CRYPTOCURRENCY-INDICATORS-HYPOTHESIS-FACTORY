# SOURCE NOTES

Important public sources consulted, access classification, dates checked, and what they do/do not establish. All sources must be freely accessible.

Access classification:
1. Confirmed free from documented public information.
2. Apparently free, but a relevant limitation remains unverified.
3. Not suitable without paid or unavailable access.

- Bank for International Settlements, Working Paper No. 1087, "Crypto carry" (2023-03-24). URL: https://www.bis.org/publications/working-paper-1087-crypto-carry.pdf. Access: 1 (confirmed free public PDF). Provides: finding that high 1-month BTC futures carry predicts future spot price crashes and sell liquidations rising as a share of open interest; crypto cash-and-carry return characteristics; limits to arbitrage deployment (absence of cross-margining, leverage caps e.g. Binance max leverage cut to 20x in July 2021); data on futures basis, volume, open interest, buy/sell liquidations, options OI and skew. Date checked: 2026-10-20. Establishes: carry is a statistically significant predictor of crashes/liquidations in-sample (roughly through Jan 2022); does not establish out-of-sample performance after 2022 or on current perpetual-venue structure.
- Chi (2023), "An empirical investigation on risk factors in cryptocurrency futures", Journal of Futures Markets. URL: https://doi.org/10.1002/fut.22425. Access: 2 (abstract freely available; full-text access behind publisher paywall not verified). Provides: basis factor strongest predictor of cross-sectional crypto futures returns 2017-2021 (basis factor ~329%/yr abnormal, t=7.10); momentum weaker; basis-momentum premium subsumed by basis; interpretation: information segmentation between spot and futures markets. Date checked: 2026-10-20. Establishes: basis has cross-sectional predictive content in futures; full regression tables and robustness claims not independently verifiable from abstract alone.
- Liu & Tsyvinski (2021), NBER Working Paper w24877, "Risks and Returns of Cryptocurrency". URL: https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24877/w24877.pdf. Access: 1 (confirmed free public PDF). Provides: time-series momentum effect; investor-attention proxies (Twitter post counts) forecast 1-2 week returns; 155 other factors with no discernible patterns. Date checked: 2026-10-20. Context only; attention data (Twitter/X API) is not free today, so not a primary input for any core hypothesis here.
- Garcia Seuma (2026), "Where does the criticality live? Early-warning signals are event-heterogeneous across seven crypto-perpetual liquidation cascades", arXiv:2607.27070. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27070. Access: 1 (confirmed free arXiv). Provides: anatomy of seven BTC perpetual liquidation cascades 2022-2025 including the 10 Oct 2025 ~$19B event; forced liquidations emit price-insensitive orders creating positive-feedback price impact; funding settles every 8 hours (too coarse for intraday warning); open interest falls 24.6% across cascades. Date checked: 2026-10-20. Establishes: forced-unwind feedback mechanism is real and documented; no single-variable early-warning signal is event-invariant.
- Garcia Seuma (2026), "Measuring the engine of a liquidation cascade: subcritical branching inside a first-order transition", arXiv:2608.03616. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03616. Access: 1 (confirmed free arXiv). Provides: branching-ratio measurement of the Oct 2025 cascade; cascade ran deeply subcritical; severity set by shock size x liquidity withdrawal, not a diverging multiplier. Date checked: 2026-10-20. Establishes: liquidation cascades are abrupt shock-driven transitions; pre-cascade early-warning claims are fragile.
- Perez et al.-style BitMEX liquidation study: "Liquidation, Leverage and Optimal Margin in Bitcoin Futures Markets" (2021-02-10), arXiv:2102.04591. URL: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.04591. Access: 1 (confirmed free arXiv PDF). Provides: daily forced liquidations substantial on BitMEX perpetuals (3.51% long / 1.89% short of outstanding futures); forced-liquidation traders used avg ~60X leverage; Black Thursday 12 Mar 2020 long liquidation peaked ~$843M. Date checked: 2026-10-20. Establishes: high leverage and forced liquidation are persistent features of perpetual markets; single-venue (BitMEX) sample ending Feb 2021.
- Gate.io Wiki, "How do derivative market signals predict crypto price movements..." (2026-01-09 article, checked 2026-10-20). URL: https://gate.com/crypto-wiki/article/how-do-derivative-market-signals-predict-crypto-price-movements-analyzing-futures-open-interest-funding-rates-and-liquidation-data-20260109. Access: 2 (freely readable; exchange-published, marketing-adjacent, claims such as 60-75% directional accuracy not independently verified). Provides: practitioner framing of rising OI + positive funding as bullish conviction; excessive OI preceding corrections via liquidation vulnerability; declining funding during price increases preceding short squeezes. Treat as anecdotal/secondary, not evidence.

No source above was used to claim profitability or validation. All are mechanism/literature references only.

- He, Manela, Ross & von Wachter (2026), "Fundamentals of Perpetual Futures", arXiv:2212.06888 (v1 Dec 2022; v7 17 Sep 2026). URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06888. Access: 1 (confirmed free arXiv, CC-BY). Provides: no-arbitrage benchmark prices for perpetual futures and bounds with trading costs; empirically, deviations from these prices in crypto are larger than in traditional currency markets, co-move across currencies, and diminish over time; funding rates tend to be similar across exchanges because of cross-exchange arbitrage but can diverge during extreme liquidity episodes; the residual cross-exchange dispersion is attributed to segmented arbitrage across venues (no cross-margining, each leg separately funded and margined) and to liquidity differences across exchanges; Binance–Bybit funding correlation of 0.74 with meaningful divergences during unusually large funding payments; past returns explain more than half of the futures–spot gap in a time-series regression, interpreted as momentum / positive-feedback trading; the authors name residual cross-exchange funding dispersion as a promising avenue for future research. Date checked: 2026-10-20. Establishes: the pricing mechanics of perpetuals, the existence and documented causes of cross-venue funding dispersion, and the momentum-based alternative explanation for the futures–spot gap; does not establish any predictive return claim for funding levels or dispersion, and the momentum result is the principal competing explanation for CRYPTO-EDGE-001 and CRYPTO-EDGE-002.
- Gornall, Rinaldi & Xiao (2025), "Perpetual Futures and Basis Risk: Evidence from Cryptocurrency" (Nov 2024; rev. 19 May 2025). URL: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5036933 (SSRN page returned HTTP 403 to automated fetch on 2026-10-20; abstract read via search index and the AEA 2026 program page https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2026/program/paper/ByyFEfr4, the latter rendering as a PDF that automated fetch could not parse). Access: 2 (abstract and program-page summary freely readable; full PDF not verified in this session). Provides: perpetuals now dominate crypto derivatives trading volume; they enhance liquidity and reduce extreme price dislocations; arbitrage drawdowns on perpetuals are about one-third the size of quarterly-futures drawdowns and mean-revert quickly while quarterly drawdowns persist; a tractable model of capital-constrained arbitrage explains the design; funding-rate mechanics keep perpetual prices near spot, reducing the fragility of spot–futures–basis trades; perps lead spot price discovery. Date checked: 2026-10-20. Establishes: structural change in the derivatives market that compresses the extreme-basis states CRYPTO-EDGE-001 depends on, and the strongest contrary argument for CRYPTO-EDGE-003 (the expensive venue may be the price leader); establishes no forward-return claim.
- Zhivkov (2026), "The Two-Tiered Structure of Cryptocurrency Funding Rate Markets", Mathematics 14(2):346, doi 10.3390/math14020346 (published 20 Jan 2026). URL: https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7390/14/2/346. Access: 1 (open access; publisher page returned HTTP 403 to automated fetch on 2026-10-20, content read via DOAJ/EconPapers/Semantic Scholar indexes and the MDPI XML rendering). Provides: 35.7 million one-minute funding observations across 26 exchanges (11 CEX, 15 DEX), 749 symbols, eight consecutive days; CEX venues dominate price discovery with 61% higher integration than DEX and information flow runs CEX→DEX with no reverse causality; 17% of observations show arbitrage spreads of at least 20 bps but only ~40% of top opportunities remain profitable after transaction costs and spread reversals, with forced exits in ~95% of opportunities; funding rates cluster near zero (IQR 1.20 bps CEX); market frictions manifest in cross-platform spreads rather than in average levels. Date checked: 2026-10-20. Establishes: persistent cross-venue funding fragmentation and the poor cost-adjusted economics of pure funding arbitrage; limitations: eight-day sample, one-minute granularity, single-author feature paper, so treated as supporting microstructure evidence, not decisive.
- Cao, Zhai & Luo (2024), "Anatomy of cryptocurrency perpetual futures returns", Journal of Futures Markets (accepted/in press 9 Apr 2024). URL: https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/en/publications/anatomy-of-cryptocurrency-perpetual-futures-returns. Access: 2 (abstract freely readable via the University of Edinburgh research record; full text access unverified). Provides: a cost-of-carry model for perpetual returns; evaluation of 134 return predictors (basis, momentum, volume, size, volatility) with 48 statistically significant strategies; a two-factor model combining basis and price-volume factors explains all 48 identified strategies. Date checked: 2026-10-20. Establishes: that basis-level information is central to perpetual futures return predictability at the factor level; the abstract alone does not support any specific crash-timing claim.
- "Implied volatility slopes and jumps in bitcoin options market", Journal of Financial Markets (2024). URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167637724000713. Access: 2 (publisher abstract readable; full text access unverified). Provides: Deribit tick-by-tick BTC options; implied-volatility left/right slopes respond to price jumps and net buying pressure; the slopes forecast realized volatility on a weekly frequency but "lack the predictive capability for returns". Date checked: 2026-10-20. Establishes: contrary evidence against options-implied-slope return predictors in crypto, and the basis for rejecting that candidate (see REJECTED_AND_DEFERRED.md).
- Schmeling, Schrimpf & Todorov (2026), "Crypto Carry", Management Science, doi 10.1287/mnsc.2024.05069 (received 27 Feb 2024; accepted 13 Nov 2025; published online 6 May 2026). URL: https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/mnsc.2024.05069. Access: 2 (abstract and metadata freely readable; full text returned HTTP 403 to automated fetch on 2026-10-20). Provides: abstract-level results that carry can exceed 40% p.a., that it traces to (i) demand from smaller trend-chasing investors seeking leveraged exposure and (ii) limited deployment of arbitrage capital because of regulatory and margin frictions, and that structural limits to arbitrage amplify price inefficiencies; data description states daily data from March 2019 to July 2024 on BTC and ETH spot prices plus futures and options characteristics. Date checked: 2026-10-20. Establishes: the published, peer-reviewed status of the carry-crash mechanism and its extended sample; does not permit independent verification of the post-2022 crash-predictive coefficient estimates from free access, which is why the score for evidence quality was held at 4/5.
- CEPR VoxEU column, Schmeling, Schrimpf & Todorov (22 Dec 2025), "Crypto carry: Market segmentation and price distortions in digital asset markets". URL: https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/crypto-carry-market-segmentation-and-price-distortions-digital-asset-markets. Access: 2 (freely readable via search index; publisher page returned HTTP 403 to automated fetch on 2026-10-20). Provides: authors' own summary stating the carry time series sample runs March 2019 to July 2024 for OKEx with data from Skew and Coinmetrics, that carry averages around 7–8% per year depending on asset and contract, and that carry is driven by smaller leveraged investors with scarce arbitrage capital on the other side. Date checked: 2026-10-20. Establishes: independent confirmation of the extended sample window used to correct the cycle 1–3 characterisation.
