# REJECTED AND DEFERRED

Rationale for rejects, duplicates, and ideas awaiting material missing input.

## Rejected (this cycle or earlier) — do not rediscover without a material new fact

- **Funding-extreme contrarian (the idea formerly catalogued as CRYPTO-EDGE-002). Rejected on 2026-10-20** (sixth cycle), after being carried in the catalogue since cycle 1. Reason: the idea's core prediction was preregistered, dated and tested by an independent party, and failed on both of its preregistered criteria with point estimates of the wrong sign. The test used this catalogue's own signal construction — trailing-90-day percentile rank of daily funding, LOW <= 10th and HIGH >= 90th, fixed before the run — on Binance USDT-margined perpetuals across 8 symbols and 17,910 symbol-days from December 2019 to July 2026, which covers the post-July-2024 window this catalogue had flagged as untested. Result: 7-day LOW-minus-HIGH = -0.95%, 95% moving-block bootstrap CI [-3.20%, +1.39%], sign positive in only 4 of 8 symbols against a required 5; HIGH-funding days were followed by +3.30% versus +2.35% at 7 days and +12.61% versus +4.83% at 30 days. The momentum-flavored variant (HIGH funding read as confirmation) was tested in the same ledger and also failed to clear its bar (+7.78%, CI [-0.77%, +17.58%] at 30 days), so the opposite sign was *not* promoted either. Combined with the absence of any published study tying funding percentiles to forward returns, the momentum explanation of the futures-spot gap (He, Manela, Ross and von Wachter 2026), and funding clustering near zero across venues (Zhivkov 2026), the supporting evidence for the registered prediction is empty. Reinstatement requires one of: an independent preregistered test on a disjoint sample and window with the correct sign and at least five of eight symbols positive; a peer-reviewed study establishing contrarian predictability from funding extremes with a mechanism that is not momentum reversal; or a documented structural change in the funding mechanism that would invalidate the failed test's mapping. The forced-liquidation *mechanism* is not what was rejected, and a volatility-forecasting use of funding is a different hypothesis (see the deferred entry below).
- **Perp-basis level as a contrarian / mean-reversion state variable. Rejected on 2026-10-20.** Reason: this is the single-instrument, cross-venue-nested sibling of the rejected funding-extreme idea, and it failed in the same direction on the same machinery. A preregistered test on Binance USDT perpetuals versus spot (17,899 symbol-days, trailing-90-day percentile of the daily basis, 10th/90th deciles fixed in advance) returned a 7-day LOW-minus-HIGH of -2.61% with a 95% block-bootstrap interval of [-4.88%, -0.12%] — the interval excludes zero on the side opposite the registered prediction — with 2 of 8 symbols positive; high-premium days were followed by +4.20% versus +1.59% at 7 days and +14.8% versus +6.1% at 30 days. The momentum-flavored version was noted in the same source as showing significance but was not the registered claim and was closed. This rejection is what drove CRYPTO-EDGE-001's restructure from a directional return claim to a crash-conditionality claim; it is recorded separately because the idea of fading an elevated perp basis is the single most likely thing to be re-proposed.
- **Cross-venue fiat-versus-offshore premium continuation as a standalone directional book. Rejected on 2026-10-20** (sixth cycle, non-derivatives coverage). Reason: the *information* signal is real and preregistered — the peg-adjusted premium of a regulated USD venue over the offshore USDT venues predicted forward returns in the continuation direction at +3.17% per 7 days, CI [+0.51%, +6.08%], breadth 17 of 20 symbols, on 31,752 symbol-days from 2019-01-01 to 2026-08-27, surviving a trailing-7-day-return trim (+2.47%, CI [+0.13%, +4.74%]) and the removal of the two largest contributors (+2.85%, CI [+0.34%, +5.46%]) — but carrying it into a strategy-grade test with next-open fills, 10bp fee, 1bp half-spread and 25bp participation impact per side was KILLED on all three gates: Sharpe 0.89 against a required 1.0, deflated Sharpe 0.36 against a required 0.95, and Sharpe below the equal-weight buy-and-hold benchmark of 0.94 computed in the same run on the same window and engine; correlation with the incumbent book +0.3929 against a 0.30 bar; 2025 and 2026 both negative. The sample was a bull market with an unconditional 7-day forward return of +0.92%, so a long-only crypto book would have passed a naive profitability bar almost regardless. Do not re-propose the same premium signal as a long-only crypto book. A *delta-neutral* cross-venue version was never tested and is not rejected by this entry, but nothing found in this cycle supports it either.
- **Stablecoin supply growth as a crypto-demand / flow proxy. Rejected on 2026-10-20** (sixth cycle, non-derivatives coverage). Reason: two independent problems. First, endogeneity in the same form as the deferred spot-ETF-flow idea — rising crypto prices create demand for stablecoins, so supply growth responds to returns as well as predicting them. Second, and newly documented, the proxy is contaminated: free DefiLlama data show total stablecoin supply above $310B by mid-2026 versus roughly $124B at end-2023, but practitioner decomposition attributes a growing share of that growth to enterprise and payments infrastructure, cross-border payments, AI-agent payments and yield-bearing stablecoins rather than to crypto trading demand, so the supply series increasingly measures fiat-tokenisation activity rather than crypto trading ammunition. The data itself is confirmed free (access 1, https://stablecoins.llama.fi/stablecoincharts/all and https://defillama.com/stablecoins); it is the *mechanism* that fails. Revisit only if a source establishes a return-residualized stablecoin-supply shock with independent price impact, or if supply can be cleanly split into trading-motive and non-trading-motive components with free data.
- **Stablecoin peg deviation (for example USDT trading at a discount) as a directional crypto return signal. Rejected on 2026-10-20** (sixth cycle, non-derivatives coverage). Reason: the closest preregistered test of a stablecoin-peg signal found it was noise at every horizon tested — +0.15% at 1 day, +0.12% at 7 days and -8.86% at 30 days, with breadth of 13/20, 10/20 and 17/20 — and, in the same test, the information was found in the *asset* dislocation rather than in the peg: the peg-adjusted venue premium signalled while the peg component did not. A directional peg signal therefore fails both its own test and the decomposition test. A peg *deviation* used as a stress or regime filter (rather than a directional signal) is not rejected, but no entry for it is proposed.
- Pure cross-venue funding arbitrage as a *return source* (delta-neutral long the low-funding venue, short the high-funding venue, harvesting the funding differential). Rejected in this form on 2026-10-20. Reason: Zhivkov (2026, Mathematics 14(2):346, open access) reports that of the top cross-venue opportunities only about 40% remain profitable after transaction costs and spread reversals, with forced exits in about 95% of cases, and that funding clusters near zero (IQR 1.20 bps for CEX venues) — the differential is usually smaller than round-trip costs. Note that published evidence now disagrees on this point: Finance Research Letters (2026) reports funding-rate arbitrage between CEX and DEX venues with superior risk-adjusted returns to holding the asset, with low correlation to the buy-and-hold return. The disagreement is unresolved; the directional relative-performance question built on the same dispersion measure survives inside CRYPTO-EDGE-003; only the carry-capture return source is rejected. **Tenth-cycle update (2026-10-20): the rejection is reinforced, and the reason is now documented rather than inferred.** The preregistered tier has since measured the *harvest* form of this trade - long spot, short the perp, hold every contract whose own funding is positive - on a point-in-time universe as well as a hindsight one: it retains 86% of its Sharpe (5.61 to 4.83) with a better drawdown, its CAGR halves (+4.36% to +2.35%), and it earns approximately zero per year in 2025-2026 in every arm, the fifth independent confirmation of that finding. The *selection* form that a threshold rule implies is documented to be the weaker of the two, monotonically, in both Sharpe and drawdown (2.27 / 2.99 / 4.06 for K = 3 / 5 / 10 against 5.60 for harvest-all over the same 34 symbols and window). So the capture version is rejected twice over - once on costs and once on a current-regime ceiling near zero - and the surviving direction of the family is the directional relative-performance question inside CRYPTO-EDGE-003, whose own incremental content is now held to a prior-art bar. No reinstatement condition is proposed for the capture form, because the tenth cycle's evidence closes the "maybe it works at scale" reading: it is not that the capture is too small to trade, it is that the premium is not being paid.
- Crypto options implied-volatility slope / skew as a *return* predictor. Rejected on 2026-10-20. Reason: published evidence (Journal of Financial Markets, 2024, "Implied volatility slopes and jumps in bitcoin options market", Deribit tick-by-tick data) finds IV slopes forecast realized volatility on a weekly frequency but "lack the predictive capability for returns"; Hoang (2020, Journal of Futures Markets) likewise finds option IV informative mainly for volatility forecasting rather than returns. A volatility-forecasting hypothesis using Deribit data would be a different idea and is not currently justified by this repository's evidence. Revisit only if a source documents return predictive content for a specific crypto option surface feature.
- Parameter-sweep variants of the existing entries (different trailing windows, decile cut-offs, or basis thresholds under new names). Not counted as new hypotheses; the catalogue keeps one preregistered specification per mechanism to avoid multiple-testing contamination.

- **Cross-sectional state-variable conditioning of the cross-sectional momentum spread, without a survivorship-aware universe. Rejected on 2026-10-20** (seventh cycle). Reason: this is the family of CRYPTO-EDGE-004, and the two closest preregistered tests of it are both killed, one of them with a point estimate leaning the opposite way to the registered prediction. The "dispersion dial" test conditions the top-2-minus-bottom-2 forward 7-day momentum spread on the cross-sectional standard deviation of trailing 30-day returns, on the wide 40-coin daily panel over 64,484 symbol-days, and is KILLED: the spread was monotone *increasing* across dispersion terciles (+0.50%, +1.05%, +1.40%) with a top-minus-bottom difference of +0.90% and a 95% block-bootstrap interval of [-2.66%, +3.69%]. The "breadth dial" sibling conditions the same book on the share of the universe with positive trailing returns and is KILLED on the same pattern (+0.90%, +1.15%, +2.92%; top-minus-bottom +2.02%, CI [-2.10%, +5.74%]). The published source of the family (Zhang and Makgolo 2026) had already warned that its own inference is sensitive to dynamic universe construction. Do not re-propose this family without a survivorship-aware universe AND a materially different state variable; the two killed preregistered tests are the prior art. This rejection does not extend to the survivorship-aware version, which is deferred below for a data reason rather than an evidence reason.
- **Flow-conditioned short-horizon reversal as a retail-scale crypto return source (the third non-derivatives family searched for in the ninth cycle). Rejected on 2026-10-20.** Reason: this family clears the free-data bar cleanly, has the strongest published result located in this cycle, and fails only on net economics. Kitron and Wengrowicz (2026, arXiv:2608.21888, open access, with public replication code and frozen results) show that at 15-minute horizons directional mean reversion is far stronger and more pervasive in crypto than in US equities: under one matched, strictly out-of-sample walk-forward protocol, 90% of 183 Binance pairs carry significant directional reversal against 2.7% of 187 US stocks and ETFs, in every focal coin-year since 2021, with a class-mean AUC gap of +0.031 as designed and +0.011 (95% CI [+0.008, +0.014]) under the most conservative accounting, surviving an artifact battery, an exact permutation null and a frozen six-month holdout. The mechanism is documented and coherent: the reversal concentrates after moves driven by aggressive taker flow and grows with flow intensity, while the order-book depth a move consumes conditions nothing, a pattern consistent with compensated liquidity provision. The inputs are free and point-in-time and were confirmed present in the same Binance public archive this cycle (daily/bookDepth/ from 2023-01-01, daily/aggTrades/ and daily/trades/). The rejection is on cost: the gross edge peaks near 1.3 bp per trade against a 5 bp round-trip cost, which the authors themselves describe as "large enough to detect, too small to clear benchmark spot capture costs". This is the cleanest external demonstration in the catalogue that detection robustness and tradability are separate axes in this asset class, and the second instance here (the first was the cross-venue carry capture) of a robustly detected signal failing only on costs. **Reinstatement condition:** an execution venue and price schedule where the round-trip cost is below roughly 1.3 bp (maker rebates, co-located or fee-tier-advantaged execution at institutional scale), or a variant of the signal whose per-trade gross is an order of magnitude larger than this one. Do not re-propose the plain "bet against the previous candle" version on any retail venue.

- **Conditional funding-extreme reversal-versus-continuation split (the sign of the funding-extreme response conditioned on the direction of the market on the event day). Deferred on 2026-10-20** (seventh cycle) rather than promoted, and recorded here so that it is not rediscovered from scratch. Reason: the published support is one unreviewed single-author preprint whose headline magnitude is far larger than anything else in this literature and therefore needs replication before it is catalogued. Shek (2026, SSRN 7520320) reports 274 funding-rate extreme events across BTC, ETH and SOL from 2021 to 2026, with events on up days reverting by +1.88% over the next 24 hours and events on down days continuing by -1.74%, a +3.62pp gap with a 95% block-bootstrap interval of [2.60, 4.67] and a pooled +6.47pp around seven exogenous shocks. A second source partially corroborates the reversal leg without the condition (Memon, Anklesaria-Dalal and Mishra 2026: extreme *negative* funding on Binance BTC perpetuals predicts a +0.506% 24-hour return, significant under t, Wilcoxon and directional-frequency tests and robust to false-discovery-rate correction, while extreme *positive* funding has no comparable effect). A third source contradicts the negative leg outright and documents cross-asset sign heterogeneity (MarketTrace, 2026: 789 days, three venues, six assets; deeply negative funding predicts nothing on any of the six, with 622 episodes and medians within +/-0.7%, while the top-decile state predicts a negative median 72-hour return on BTC and positive medians on ETH, BNB, XRP and DOGE). The signal is also closely adjacent to the already-rejected CRYPTO-EDGE-002, and the conditional form is a narrowing of a claim whose unconditional form was tested and failed, so it must clear a higher bar before it is promoted, not a lower one. **Reinstatement condition:** an independent preregistered test of the conditional split on a disjoint sample and window, with the event-day conditioning applied at a tradable decision time, and with the positive and negative tails reported separately for each asset. A companion result worth reading alongside it: a second paper by the same author (SSRN 7529458) documents that the *speed* of correction after a funding extreme is dominated by realized volatility (median 27.0 hours at high volatility against 3.0 hours at low), and a third (SSRN 7514718) documents that an unconditional funding-mean-reversion strategy earns Sharpe 1.248 on BTC but -0.084 on ETH and -0.879 on SOL, which is a cross-asset sign instability that any candidate in this family must confront. **Tenth-cycle update (2026-10-20): still not replicated, and the adjacent preregistered tier now points the other way.** No independent replication or refutation of Shek (2026) was located in the tenth cycle either, so the deferral stands on the same single-source ground. What changed is the neighbourhood: a preregistered test in the same tier finds that in a crypto panel the *asymmetry* form this headline rests on is estimable with the machinery the catalogue requires (block bootstrap with the cross-section intact within 30-day date blocks), and it finds the direction to be the opposite one - extreme *up* shocks continue rather than revert, at +3.54% over the next 7 days (CI [+1.25%, +5.99%], n = 924), with moderate and extreme down shocks indistinguishable from noise, the same ledger calling it the fourth independent test in crypto where continuation beats reversal. That is not a replication of Shek's funding-extreme result (different conditioning variable, different asset panel) and it is not evidence against it either; it is evidence that the statistical form is attainable in crypto and that the direction in the nearest adjacent test is continuation, which lowers the prior on Shek's reversal headline and raises the bar the reinstatement condition must clear. The reinstatement condition is unchanged and now also requires a replication to state which direction it expects, so that a reversal result and a continuation result cannot both be read as support. **Eleventh-cycle update (2026-10-20): the deferral is UNCHANGED and one mandatory control has been added; the single-source problem is now sharper, not weaker.** The eleventh cycle searched specifically for an independent replication or refutation of the funding-extreme conditional split — a preregistered test, by a party other than Shek, of whether the *sign* of the post-funding-extreme response depends on the market's direction that day — and **found none**. The nearest adjacent preregistered result still points the other way (extreme *up* shocks continue at +3.54% over the next 7 days, CI [+1.25%, +5.99%], n = 924), and the catalogue's own record still notes that a preregistered test in the same tier attributes the same outcome to *shock asymmetry* rather than to funding-specific content. What the eleventh cycle adds is a **mandatory control** and a **sharper statement of what the single source actually says**. (i) *Mandatory control.* The speed of correction after a funding extreme is documented to be dominated by **realised volatility** rather than by the funding level (Shek's own second paper, SSRN 7529458, on 1,696 funding events across 18 Binance perpetual contracts, with a correction time of 27.0 hours in high-volatility regimes against 3.0 hours in low-volatility regimes). Any replication of the *sign split* must therefore condition on realised volatility first; a sign difference between up days and down days that is really a volatility difference between up days and down days is the obvious confound, and the replication must pre-commit to that control, to the volatility measure, and to the window over which it is measured. (ii) *The single-source problem is sharper because the source is now better characterised.* The original result is registered on **274 funding-rate extreme events across BTC, ETH and SOL from 2021 to 2026**, with up-day events reverting by +1.88% over the next 24 hours, down-day events continuing by -1.74%, a gap of +3.62 percentage points (block bootstrap 95% CI [2.60, 4.67]), a net-of-funding gap of +3.59 percentage points, a positive gap in all seven of the seven exogenous market-wide shock windows (sign test p = 0.00781) with a pooled estimate of +6.47 percentage points (95% CI [4.45, 8.86]), and consistency across 12 year-by-direction cells and 13 coins. A replication must therefore reproduce a specific and *small* event count with a *specific* conditioning variable, and the catalogue's requirement that a replication **state its expected direction in advance** is now sharpened into: a replication must pre-commit to the direction, the event definition, the volatility control, the direction-conditioning rule, and the 24-hour horizon, and must report the event count it obtained, because a replication that reclassifies events until the sign appears is the failure mode this condition exists to prevent. (iii) *One new adjacent finding is favourable to the family and is recorded as such.* The AFT 2026 conference paper's finding that **front-end (retail) traders are 96.0% of three-group liquidation volume** (97.6% excluding October 10, 2025), and that forced liquidations impose a $344 million loss on front-end traders against which auto-deleveraging returns $118 million, is a *mechanism* fact consistent with a structural rather than noise-driven asymmetry: if the forced-unwind side of the market is overwhelmingly retail, then a funding extreme that precedes a forced unwind is a retail-positioning event, and conditioning on the market's direction is conditioning on whether the retail unwind is already underway.
